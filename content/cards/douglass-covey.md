On 1 January 1833 Frederick Douglass, a boy of about sixteen, was sent to work
for Edward Covey, a farmer near St. Michael's, Maryland, known as a
"negro-breaker". Whippings followed. One August day Douglass
collapsed while fanning wheat, fled, and was sent back. When Covey tried to tie
him in the stable, Douglass seized him by the throat. They fought, he wrote,
"for nearly two hours", and Covey never laid a hand on him again. In his 1845
*Narrative* he introduced the scene with one sentence: "You have seen how a man
was made a slave; you shall see how a slave was made a man." It was, he wrote,
"the turning-point in my career as a slave". In 1838 he escaped north. Compare [[jacobs-garret]].
