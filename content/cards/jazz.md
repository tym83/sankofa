On Sunday afternoons enslaved Africans gathered in New Orleans' Congo
Square to drum, sing and dance, in public longer and later than anywhere
else in North America. Later, from about 1898 to 1906, the cornetist Buddy
Bolden ruled Black New Orleans with a band now seen as the first to play
jazz, full of the [[blues]]. No recording of him exists. The first jazz
record, "Livery Stable Blues" (Victor, 1917), was cut by the white Original
Dixieland Jass Band, who claimed they had invented jazz rather than
credit their teachers. From 1925 in Chicago, Louis Armstrong's Hot Five
and Hot Seven turned jazz into a soloist's art. Armstrong had come
north in 1922, when King Oliver sent for him, part of the migration the
[[chicago-defender]] urged.
