Nanny led the Windward Maroons of Jamaica's Blue Mountains during the First
Maroon War against the British, from about 1720 to 1739. British accounts
record their fear of the guerrilla ambushes in the hills; Maroon memory adds
legends, such as Nanny catching bullets, that no document supports. When
Captain Quao signed a peace treaty in 1739, she reportedly opposed it. What is
on paper: a land patent of 1740, still in the Jamaica Archives, granting 500
acres to "Nanny" and her people. Jamaica made her its first and only woman
National Hero, and she is on the 500-dollar note, with a face invented by an
art school competition. Compare [[palmares]] and [[yaa-asantewaa]].
