On the night of 1 June 1863 three gunboats left Beaufort with Black soldiers
of the 2nd South Carolina Volunteers, Colonel James Montgomery and Harriet
Tubman. She had recruited the scouts and boatmen who knew the river. One boat
ran aground; the John Adams and the Harriet A. Weed reached the
plantations on the Combahee at dawn. While soldiers burned plantations, people ran
to the boats, and Tubman got them aboard. Counts differ: more than 700, or
750, were freed. The National Archives calls her the only woman to lead a
military action in the war. In 1974 Black feminists in Boston took their
name from the raid: the Combahee River Collective. Compare
[[robert-smalls]] and [[underground-railroad-myths]].
