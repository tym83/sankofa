In June 1863 Harriet Tubman went to war by river, and hundreds walked out of slavery. On the night of 1 June three gunboats left Beaufort carrying Black soldiers of the 2nd South Carolina Volunteers, Colonel James Montgomery and Tubman, bound for the plantations on the Combahee River. By the time they turned back, more than 700 people, or 750 by other counts, were free. In 1974 Black feminists in Boston took their name from the raid: the Combahee River Collective.
