At a women's rights convention in Akron, Ohio, in 1851, Sojourner Truth rose and
spoke. Her friend Marius Robinson printed her words in the *Anti-Slavery
Bugle* that June: "I am a woman's rights. I have as much muscle as any man, and
can do as much work as any man." There is no "Ain't I a woman?" in it. That
refrain arrived in 1863, when Frances Gage published her own version and gave
Truth the dialect of an enslaved Southerner. But Truth was born enslaved in
Ulster County, New York, and grew up speaking Low Dutch; sold at nine, she
could not understand her new English-speaking mistress. In 1864 her portrait
cards carried her own caption: "I sell the shadow to support the substance." Compare [[wpa-narratives]] and
[[underground-railroad-myths]].
