Sojourner Truth's most famous line is missing from the only report of her speech printed at the time. At a women's rights convention in Akron, Ohio, in 1851, Truth rose and spoke. Marius Robinson printed her words in the Anti-Slavery Bugle that June: "I am a woman's rights." There is no "Ain't I a woman?" in it. That refrain arrived in 1863, when Frances Gage published her own version and gave Truth the dialect of an enslaved Southerner. But Truth was born enslaved in Ulster County, New York, and grew up speaking Low Dutch.
