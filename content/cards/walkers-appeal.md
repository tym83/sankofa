David Walker, born in Wilmington, North Carolina, to a free mother, ran a clothing business
on Brattle Street in Boston. In 1829 he
published his *Appeal ... to the Coloured Citizens of the World*, a furious
call to resist slavery, its pages loud with pointing hands and exclamation
marks. He praised "Hayti, the glory of the blacks and terror of tyrants" (see
[[haitian-revolution]]). He asked sailors heading south to carry copies, and
sewed the *Appeal* into the linings of clothes he sold. South Carolina,
Georgia, North Carolina and Louisiana answered with laws making it a capital
crime to possess or spread it, and with new bans on teaching Black people to
read. According to Henry Highland Garnet, Georgians put a price on Walker's
head. He died in Boston in 1830.
