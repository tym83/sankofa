Two players meet in the middle of a circle, a fight and a dance at once,
while the single-string berimbau sets the pace and everyone else sings and
claps. In a plate published in 1835, Rugendas showed Black men playing it in Rio. The young
republic's penal code of 1890 punished capoeiragem, displays of agility and bodily
dexterity in streets and squares, with two to six months in
prison. In Salvador in 1932 Mestre Bimba opened the first formal
school, teaching his capoeira regional, officially recognised by 1937; Mestre
Pastinha kept the older Angola style. In 2014 UNESCO inscribed the capoeira
circle as intangible heritage. Compare [[palmares]], [[candomble]] and
[[samba]].
