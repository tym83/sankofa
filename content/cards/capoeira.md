In 1890 it could get you two to six months in prison; in 2014 UNESCO called it the heritage of humanity. Capoeira looks like this: two players meet in the middle of a circle, a fight and a dance at once, while the single-string berimbau sets the pace and everyone else sings and claps. Brazil's 1890 penal code punished capoeiragem, displays of agility in streets and squares. The art outlived the ban in teachers: Mestre Bimba's academy is often called the first formal school, though accounts differ on who opened the first and when, while Mestre Pastinha kept the older Angola style. In 2014 UNESCO inscribed the capoeira circle as intangible heritage. Compare [[palmares|Quilombo dos Palmares]], [[candomble|the candomblé houses of Salvador]] and [[samba|samba]].
