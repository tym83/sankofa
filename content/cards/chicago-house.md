In 1977 Frankie Knuckles moved from New York into the Warehouse, a club at
206 South Jefferson Street where young, mostly Black and gay men danced in
a segregated city. He first saw the name on a shop sign: "house music",
which a friend explained as what Knuckles played at the Warehouse. On 12 July
1979 radio DJ Steve Dahl blew up disco records at Comiskey Park. Vince Lawrence, a
Black 14-year-old usher, saw records by Black artists who were not disco
at all; someone broke a Marvin Gaye record in his face. A few years later Lawrence co-wrote "On and On", Jesse
Saunders's track that helped start house. In 2004 Chicago named a stretch of
Jefferson Street Frankie Knuckles Way. Cousins: [[detroit-techno]],
[[planet-rock]], [[funk]].
