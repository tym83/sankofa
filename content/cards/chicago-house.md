House is dance music built on a kick drum that lands on every beat of the bar, "four on the floor", at around 120 to 130 beats a minute. It was made on drum machines such as Roland's TR-808 and TR-909, with synthesizers and samples, and it grew straight out of disco: Frankie Knuckles called it "disco's revenge". Its makers were young Chicagoans, many of them Black and queer, and it was music for the dance floor of the clubs that welcomed them.

By Knuckles's own account, the genre was named after his club: he saw "house music" on a shop sign, and a friend explained it meant what he played at the Warehouse. Others also claim the name. In 1977 Knuckles moved from New York into the Warehouse, at 206 South Jefferson Street, where young, mostly Black and gay men danced, and he reworked disco and funk records there, adding a drum machine. On 12 July 1979 radio DJ Steve Dahl blew up disco records at Comiskey Park; whether Disco Demolition was racist and homophobic is still argued, though Dahl denies it.

Chicago kept dancing. By the late 1980s house had crossed to Britain and was on its way around the world, and in 2004 Chicago named a stretch of Jefferson Street Frankie Knuckles Way. Cousins: [[detroit-techno]], [[planet-rock]], [[funk]].
