Amos Tutuola, born in Abeokuta in 1920, left school after his father's death
and worked as a messenger at the Department of Labour in Lagos, writing in
the quiet hours. His book follows a man who drinks palm wine all day and,
when his tapster dies, walks to Deads' Town to bring him back, past creatures
from Yoruba tales told at the hearth. Faber published it in London in 1952. Dylan Thomas, in The Observer,
was wildly enthusiastic. Many Nigerian readers were not: they called the
English broken and the stories borrowed, a quarrel that is still contested.
The argument over whose English an African writer should use ran on through
[[achebe]] and [[ngugi]].
