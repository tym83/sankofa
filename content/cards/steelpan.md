A steelpan is a tuned drum traditionally made from a 55-gallon oil drum. Its playing surface is marked out and grooved into separate patches, and the tuner hammers each patch until it rings at a fixed pitch, so a single pan plays a whole scale of notes. In a steelband the high tenor, or lead, pans carry the melody, mid-range pans fill in like the guitars and cellos of an orchestra, and the bass pans hold the bottom. Steelbands rehearse in neighbourhood panyards and meet at Carnival in Panorama, the national competition.

Young men in Port of Spain took discarded oil drums and hammered them into tuned notes, and an orchestra was born from scrap. Before metal there was tamboo bamboo, played by striking lengths of bamboo. Winston "Spree" Simon and Ellie Mannette were among the pan pioneers. In July 1951 the Trinidad All Steel Percussion Orchestra played the Festival of Britain and changed minds, and in 2024 Parliament voted the steelpan the national instrument. In London Russ Henderson's steelband was central to the early outdoor Notting Hill events and also played [[claudia-jones|Claudia Jones]]'s indoor carnivals.
