After the Canboulay riots of the 1880s, colonial Trinidad banned unlicensed
drums at Carnival. People answered with tamboo bamboo, lengths of bamboo
struck together or on the ground. By the 1930s bamboo was banned too, and
young men in Laventille, Belmont and St. James beat biscuit tins, paint tins
and finally discarded 55-gallon oil drums, hammering them into tuned notes.
Winston "Spree" Simon and Ellie Mannette were among the pioneers. Pan first
meant gang trouble to respectable Trinidad; in July 1951 the Trinidad All
Steel Percussion Orchestra played the Festival of Britain and changed minds.
In 2024 Parliament voted it the national instrument. In London Russ Henderson's was the first
steelband at Notting Hill Carnival, heir to [[claudia-jones]]'s indoor carnivals.
