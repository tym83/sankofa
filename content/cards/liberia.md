In 1930 an international commission found officials of Liberia, a republic founded by Black American settlers, shipping forced labour to Fernando Po. In 1816 white Americans had founded the American Colonization Society to move free Black people to Africa; its first settlers sailed in 1820, and editors like John Russwurm of [[freedoms-journal]] followed. In 1847 Liberia declared itself a sovereign republic. The settlers mostly kept apart from the peoples already there and filled the government. The commission, with the African American scholar Charles S. Johnson as the U.S. member, named Vice President Yancy and called the conditions "scarcely distinguishable from slave raiding". President King resigned. Compare [[haitian-revolution|Haiti]] and the [[vai-script]].
