In 1816 white Americans founded the American Colonization Society to move
free Black people to Africa. Its first settlers sailed in 1820; in 1821 a
U.S. Navy officer coerced a local ruler into selling land near what became
Monrovia. Newspaper editors like John Russwurm of [[freedoms-journal]]
followed. In 1847 Liberia declared itself a sovereign republic, the second
Black one after [[haitian-revolution|Haiti]]. The settlers mostly kept apart
from the peoples already there, among them the Vai of the [[vai-script]],
and filled the government. In 1930 an international commission, with the African American
scholar Charles S. Johnson as the U.S. member, found Vice President
Yancy and other officials shipping forced labour to Fernando Po in
conditions it called scarcely distinguishable from slave raiding. Facing
impeachment, President King resigned.
