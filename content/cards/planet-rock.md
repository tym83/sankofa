In 1982 Afrika Bambaataa, Bronx DJ and founder of the Universal Zulu
Nation, cut a record for Tom Silverman's new label, Tommy Boy, with producer
Arthur Baker. Bambaataa already played Kraftwerk's "Trans-Europe Express"
in his sets; Baker loved the beat of "Numbers". They rebuilt both on a
Roland TR-808, without sampling. Kraftwerk were angry, and Tommy Boy paid
them a dollar a record. In Detroit, Juan Atkins was mixing Kraftwerk with
Funkadelic too; [[detroit-techno]] then crossed to Berlin, and the German
sound came home. From 2016 several men accused Bambaataa of abusing them as
boys. He denied it, the Zulu Nation removed its accused leaders, and in
2025 he lost a civil suit by default. He died in 2026. See [[hip-hop]],
[[funk]], [[chicago-house]].
