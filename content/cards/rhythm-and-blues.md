Billboard ranked Black popular records under names like "Harlem Hit
Parade", "Sepia" and "Race". Jerry Wexler, a young writer editing its
charts, proposed "rhythm and blues", and in June 1949 the magazine renamed
the chart. The name covered many styles, cut at first by small independent labels
in Los Angeles, then in New York. Louis Jordan topped
the R&B charts for 113 weeks in all; his "Saturday Night Fish Fry" is
sometimes called the first rock and roll record. Atlantic Records,
founded in 1947, became known as "The House that Ruth Built" thanks to Ruth Brown,
yet she paid her own touring and recording costs and left with no savings.
Out of R&B, the [[blues]] and [[gospel]] came [[rock-n-roll]] and
[[funk]].
