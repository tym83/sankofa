The story goes like this: hold a brown paper bag to someone's arm, and if
their skin is darker, they are not let into the club, the fraternity or the
sorority. The scholar Audrey Elisa Kerr spent years from 1993 asking Black
Washington whether the test was real or an urban myth. Many refused to talk;
churches opened their photo albums. At 15th Street Presbyterian Church the
old pictures show lighter members in the front pews and darker ones further
back. The bag itself remains contested; the preference it names is measurable.
In North Carolina, a 2011 Villanova study of more than 12,000 imprisoned Black
women found those perceived as light-skinned got sentences about 12% shorter.
