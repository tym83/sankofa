The people France had enslaved defeated Napoleon, and then France sent them
a bill. After a ceremony at Bois Caïman, the enslaved of northern
Saint-Domingue rose in August 1791. Under Toussaint Louverture they fought
off British and Spanish armies. Napoleon tried to restore slavery in 1802;
Jean-Jacques Dessalines beat his troops and on 1 January 1804 proclaimed
Haiti. In 1825 France demanded an indemnity of 150 million francs, later
cut to 90 million, to compensate the former planters; in 2022 the New York
Times traced the payments over decades. A contested thesis by Susan
Buck-Morss holds that Hegel built his master and slave out of Haitian news.
Compare the maroons of [[palmares]] and [[nanny-of-the-maroons|Jamaica]],
and [[general-dumas]].
