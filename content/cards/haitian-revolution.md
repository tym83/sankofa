After a ceremony at Bois Caïman, the enslaved of northern Saint-Domingue, the
richest colony in the French empire, rose in August 1791. Under Toussaint
Louverture they fought off French, British and Spanish armies. Napoleon tried
to restore slavery in 1802; Jean-Jacques Dessalines beat his troops, and on
1 January 1804 proclaimed Haiti. In 1825 Charles X sent warships to demand an
indemnity of 150 million francs, later cut to 90 million, to compensate the
former planters; the New York Times series The Ransom (2022) traced the payments
over decades. A contested thesis by Susan Buck-Morss (2000) holds that Hegel
built his master and slave out of Haitian news, which makes renaming "master"
in software less abstract than it looks. Compare the maroons of [[palmares]]
and [[nanny-of-the-maroons|Jamaica]].
