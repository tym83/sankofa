In the fall of 1938 a Pentecostal singer with a guitar walked onto the
stage of the Cotton Club in Manhattan and made her first Decca records, among them
"Rock Me". In 1944 she and Sammy Price released "Strange Things Happening
Every Day", a number two on the "race" chart now heard as proto-rock. In 1947 in Macon, as the
story goes, she heard a 15-year-old Little Richard sing and put him on
her show. In May 1964, in the rain at a disused Manchester railway station,
she played electric guitar for Granada TV. She died in 1973, and her
Philadelphia grave stayed unmarked until 2009. In 2018 the Rock & Roll
Hall of Fame inducted her for musical influence. See [[gospel]] and [[rock-n-roll]].
