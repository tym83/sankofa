Octavia Estelle Butler grew up in Pasadena, raised by a mother who worked
as a maid, and was told that science fiction was no career for a Black person. Kindred (1979) brought slavery into science fiction
through time travel; Parable of the Sower (1993) raised questions about
the environment, technology and faith. In 1995 she became the first science
fiction writer to receive a MacArthur Fellowship. Her papers came to the Huntington Library by bequest
in 2008. Among them are notes to herself in which she willed her novels onto
the bestseller lists and promised to help poor Black youngsters reach
writing workshops and college. In 2020 Parable of the Sower made the New York
Times list for the first time. Read her beside [[sun-ra]] and [[hurston]].
