Half a century after the United States banned the slave trade, captives were still smuggled in, and they answered by building a town of their own. In 1860 the Mobile shipbuilder Timothy Meaher organised the voyage. Captain William Foster sailed the schooner Clotilda to Ouidah in Dahomey and bought, by most counts, 110 men, women and children. Back in Alabama, Foster burned the ship to hide the evidence. Freed in 1865, the survivors reunited, bought land and built African Town. In 1927 [[hurston|Zora Neale Hurston]] interviewed Oluale Kossola, called Cudjo Lewis; her book Barracoon waited until 2018. In May 2019 the Alabama Historical Commission identified the wreck.
