In 1860 Timothy Meaher, a Mobile shipbuilder, wagered that he could smuggle
a cargo of captives into Mobile Bay. Captain William Foster sailed the
schooner Clotilda to Ouidah in Dahomey and bought 110 men, women and children.
Back in Alabama, Foster burned the ship to hide the evidence. Freed in 1865,
the survivors reunited, bought land and built African Town,
keeping their names and Yoruba, Fon and other languages. In 1927 the young
[[hurston|Zora Neale Hurston]] interviewed Oluale Kossola, called Cudjo Lewis;
her book Barracoon waited until 2018. In May 2019 the Alabama Historical
Commission identified the wreck in the Mobile River. Compare
[[aro-and-dahomey]] and [[slavevoyages]].
