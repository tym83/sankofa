The sharpest critics of misogyny in [[hip-hop]] came from inside it. In 1991 Dr. Dre assaulted hip-hop journalist Dee Barnes at a
nightclub over the way her TV show had covered N.W.A. In 1993 Queen
Latifah's "U.N.I.T.Y." called out domestic violence, misogyny and the
objectification of Black women, and won a Grammy. In 2004 Spelman students
led by Moya Bailey, president of the Feminist Majority Leadership
Alliance, and Asha Jennings protested Nelly's "Tip Drill" video when his
foundation planned a bone-marrow drive there, and planned to join the
donor registry themselves. In 2010 Bailey wrote down a name for what they had fought: misogynoir. In 2015,
after Barnes wrote that the film Straight Outta Compton left her out, Dre
publicly apologised to the women he had hurt. See [[cindy-campbell]], [[planet-rock]].
