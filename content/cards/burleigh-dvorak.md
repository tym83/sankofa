Harry Burleigh, a grandson of a man who bought his own freedom, came from
Erie to Antonín Dvořák's National Conservatory in New York in 1892. He
copied parts, played timpani in the orchestra and, after supper, sang the
Czech composer the old plantation songs; nobody yet called them
spirituals, he later recalled. In May 1893 Dvořák told the New York Herald that the
future music of America must be founded on "Negro melodies". His
"New World" Symphony premiered that December. Burleigh went on to set
spirituals for voice and piano like European art songs, above all "Deep
River" (1916–17), and concert halls took up the [[spirituals]]. Another
Black musician between Europe and its classics: [[bridgetower]].
