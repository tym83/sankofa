Before the "New World" Symphony, there was a private concert: Harry T. Burleigh singing the old plantation songs, the spirituals, to Antonín Dvořák. Burleigh had come from Erie to Dvořák's National Conservatory in New York in 1892. The symphony premiered in December 1893. Burleigh said Dvořák quoted "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" in it, though how directly the music borrows from spirituals is still debated. Burleigh then did something new: he set spirituals for voice and piano like European art songs, above all "Deep River" (1916–17), and carried the [[spirituals]] into concert halls. Another Black musician between Europe and its classics: [[bridgetower]]; the songs still heard in church: [[gospel]].
