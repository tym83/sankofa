In the household of the future Duke of Sessa, an enslaved Black boy went with
his young master to grammar classes in Granada and became the star of the class.
He took a bachelor's degree at the new University of Granada in 1546, renamed
himself Latino and went on to teach there. In 1573 (or 1572) a Granada printer issued
his Latin verse, led by the Austrias Carmen, an epic on Don John of Austria's
victory at Lepanto. Elizabeth Wright calls him Europe's first known Black
poet. In 1605 Cervantes, in the clipped prefatory verses of Don Quixote, told
his own book not to show off Latin, since heaven had not made it as clever
(ladino) as "el negro Juan Lati-". His birthplace is disputed too. Compare [[amo]] and
[[saint-georges]].
