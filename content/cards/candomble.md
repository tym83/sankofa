Salvador's most famous candomblé temples share a mother. Behind the Barroquinha church, around 1830 (some accounts say earlier), a house for the orixás was founded. Its Yoruba name is Ilê Axé Iyá Nassô Oká; Bahians call it Casa Branca. From it descend the Gantois, led by Mãe Menininha, and the Ilê Axé Opô Afonjá. In 1984 the national heritage council voted to list it, the first terreiro ever so protected. When the American anthropologist Ruth Landes worked in Salvador's houses in 1938–39, she found them led by women, and called her 1947 book The City of Women.
