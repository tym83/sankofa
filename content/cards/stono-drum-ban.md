On Sunday 9 September 1739, near the Stono River south of Charleston,
enslaved men, several of them from Catholic Kongo, broke into a store,
took guns and marched south toward Spanish Florida, hoping to claim
freedom there. Some accounts say they beat drums and shouted "Liberty!". Their
numbers grew to perhaps a hundred before the militia crushed them. In 1740
the colony answered with the Negro Act. Besides tightening control,
it fined masters who let enslaved people "beat drums, blow horns, or use
any other loud instruments". In Charleston it was enforced; in the
countryside drums were often tolerated. Dance historians argue the
rhythm moved into the body: patting juba, hambone, and later tap and
stepping. See [[banjo]] and [[talking-drums]].
