Around 1970 the agronomist Jean Dominique bought a Port-au-Prince
station and, with Michèle Montas, his wife and co-anchor, made it the first
in Haiti to deliver news and commentary in Creole, not only in French. On 28
November 1980 the Duvalier regime raided it; the couple went into exile and
came back in March 1986 to a large crowd. A year later the new constitution
declared that all Haitians are united by one language, Creole, now official
beside French. On 3 April 2000 Dominique was shot dead outside the station. Threats closed the station by 2003. Its tapes, back to 1957, are open online at Duke. For the
country it reported on, start with the [[haitian-revolution]]; for another
Caribbean voice in exile, [[claudia-jones]].
