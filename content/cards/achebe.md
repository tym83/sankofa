Things Fall Apart nearly disappeared in the post. Achebe, born in Ogidi in eastern Nigeria in 1930, was working in Lagos when he mailed his handwritten manuscript to a London typing agency, and the agency went silent. Angela Beattie, his broadcasting supervisor, visited it while on leave and demanded that the typed manuscript be posted to Lagos. Heinemann printed 2,000 copies in 1958. He took the title from Yeats's "The Second Coming" and wrote out of indignation at Joyce Cary's Mister Johnson, to tell an Igbo village's story from the inside. In 1962 Heinemann built the African Writers Series around it, with Achebe as editorial adviser for ten years. In 1975 at Amherst he called Conrad's Heart of Darkness racist. Compare [[tutuola]] before him and [[ngugi]] after.
