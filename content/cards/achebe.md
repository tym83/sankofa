Achebe, born in Ogidi in eastern Nigeria in 1930, was working in Lagos
when he mailed his handwritten novel to a London typing agency. It went
silent; his boss, Angela Beattie, collected it while on leave. Heinemann
printed 2,000 copies in June 1958. He took the title from Yeats's "The Second
Coming" and wrote out of indignation at Joyce Cary's Mister Johnson, to tell
an Igbo village's story from the inside. Heinemann then built
the African Writers Series around it in 1962, with Achebe as editorial adviser
for ten years. In 1975 at Amherst he called Conrad's Heart of Darkness racist,
a lecture critics still argue with. Compare [[tutuola]] before him and
[[ngugi]], who later left English altogether.
