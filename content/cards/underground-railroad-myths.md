Two beloved stories lack documents. The quilt code, signals sewn into quilt
patterns hung out one by one, comes from one family's oral account in the book Hidden in
Plain View (1998). Historian Giles R. Wright and quilt historians found no
mention of it in 19th-century slave narratives or the 1930s interviews.
"Follow the Drinking Gourd" as a sung map from Mobile to the Ohio was first
printed by H. B. Parks in 1928; the route reading is contested. The real codes
were spoken plainly afterwards: in 1855 [[douglass-covey|Frederick Douglass]]
wrote that singing "O Canaan, sweet Canaan" meant "the north was our Canaan"
(see [[spirituals]]). And the network left records: William Still of the
Philadelphia Vigilance Committee logged hundreds of fugitives and published
them in 1872. Compare [[combahee-raid]].
