A shell gathered on coconut-leaf mats in the Maldives ended up as the price of human beings in West Africa. Islanders laid the mats on the water, let the molluscs settle, then dried them on the beach until only shells remained; sailors took them to the great market of Bengal. Europeans bought cowries mainly for West Africa, where buyers preferred them. Between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries ships carried them to the Bight of Benin, and cowries were paid directly for captives. Follow the ships in [[slavevoyages]] and the sellers in [[aro-and-dahomey]]; for another African fortune, see [[mansa-musa]].
