The money cowrie is a small shell that served as currency across the
Indian Ocean world for thousands of years. In the Maldives women laid mats
of coconut leaves on the water, let the molluscs settle, then dried them on
the beach until only shells remained; sailors took them to the great
market of Bengal. In the 1550s cowries were still a new commodity for
Europeans, bought mainly for West Africa, where buyers preferred them.
Portuguese, English, French and Dutch traders made them the currency of
choice for gold, goods and people. Billions were shipped to the Bight of
Benin between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries, and cowries were paid
directly for captives throughout the sea-borne trade. Follow the ships in
[[slavevoyages]] and the sellers in [[aro-and-dahomey]].
