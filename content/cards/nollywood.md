Kenneth Nnebue learned trade in the Onitsha market and by the late 1980s
was in Lagos, importing VHS tapes from Taiwan. When the tapes stopped
selling, the story goes, he put an original one on them, written with
Okechukwu Ogunjiofor. Living in Bondage (1992), shot in
Igbo for 150,000 naira, sold, by different counts, 100,000 copies in weeks or over a
million, through markets in Lagos, Enugu and beyond, and is remembered as the first
Nollywood blockbuster. Yet Nnebue had already made 27 Yoruba-language
films; the Igbo one is the one Nigeria remembers. By 2006 Nigeria was making
872 video features a year, behind India's 1,091 and ahead of the United
States' 485, a 2009 UNESCO survey found. Compare [[achebe]] and
[[tutuola]] in print, and the Lagos of [[afrobeat]].
