Offered a floor mat while the Portuguese governor sat in a chair, Njinga had
an attendant kneel and sat on them, level with him. It was 1622: she, the
sister of the king of Ndongo, had come to Luanda to negotiate peace. She
went on to take the kingdom of Matamba, allied with the Dutch when they
seized Luanda in the 1640s, and fought the Portuguese until her death in
1663. She was also a slave trader who sold war captives to the Portuguese.
Historians still argue over her reputation, but in Angola she is a national
heroine. Compare [[yaa-asantewaa|Yaa Asantewaa]] and
[[nanny-of-the-maroons|Nanny of the Maroons]]; for the trade itself, see
[[aro-and-dahomey|the African side of the trade, in Dahomey and among the
Aro]].
