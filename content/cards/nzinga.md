In 1622 Njinga, sister of the king of Ndongo, went to Portuguese Luanda to
negotiate peace. The governor sat in a chair and left her a mat; she had
an attendant kneel and used the attendant as a seat, level with him. From 1624
until her death in 1663 she fought the Portuguese, outmanoeuvred ten
governors, took the kingdom of Matamba and allied with the Dutch when
they seized Luanda in the 1640s. She was also a slave trader: according
to Linda Heywood she sold some 200,000 captives, many taken in her wars,
to the Portuguese, like her European and African rivals. In Angola she is a national heroine, and in Brazil Black
communities still sing and dance her name. Compare [[yaa-asantewaa]] and
[[nanny-of-the-maroons]]; the trade itself is in [[aro-and-dahomey]].
