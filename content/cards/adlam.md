In 1989 in Nzérékoré, Guinea, Ibrahima Barry, 14, and his brother
Abdoulaye, 10, shut themselves in their room, closed their eyes and drew
shapes on paper. Then they picked the shapes they liked and matched each to
a sound of Fulfulde, the Fulani language, which had mostly been written in
Arabic letters, sometimes Latin. The script has 28 letters and runs right
to left. Its name comes from its first four, 𞤀𞤁𞤂𞤃 (A, D, L, M), and is also an
acronym for a phrase meaning "the alphabet that protects my people from
vanishing". Unicode encoded it in version 9.0 in 2016, and Microsoft later
put it into Windows and Office. Compare [[nko]], [[vai-script]],
[[bamum]] and [[nsibidi]].
