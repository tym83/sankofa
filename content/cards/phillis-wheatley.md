In September 1773 a London bookseller published *Poems on Various Subjects,
Religious and Moral* by Phillis, a young woman brought from Africa as a child
and enslaved in a Boston household. The book opens with an
attestation in which eighteen of Boston's most prominent men, from Governor
Thomas Hutchinson to John Hancock, assured the world the poems were "written by
Phillis". Her portrait on the frontispiece is credited to Scipio Moorhead,
enslaved by a Boston minister, and the book includes her poem "To S. M. a young
African Painter, on seeing his Works". In 1775 she sent George Washington a
poem; in February 1776 he replied that if she came to Cambridge he would be
"happy to see a person so favourd by the Muses". Compare [[equiano]].
