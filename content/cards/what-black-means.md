When Brazil's 1976 household survey let people name their own colour, it
recorded 136 different terms, from morena to clara. The census today offers
just five: branca, preta, parda, amarela and indígena. Virginia had drawn
the line the other way: its Racial Integrity Act of 1924 called white a
person with "no trace whatsoever of any blood other than Caucasian", with a
small allowance for descendants of Pocahontas. And growing up in Nigeria,
Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie thought of herself as Igbo; identity there, she
says, was ethnic and religious, and she became Black in America. One word,
three worlds. Compare [[paper-bag-test|the paper bag test]], [[gannibal|Abram Petrovich Gannibal]] and
[[carolina-maria-de-jesus|Carolina Maria de Jesus]].
