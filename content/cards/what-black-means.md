Virginia's Racial Integrity Act of 1924 called white a person with "no
trace whatsoever of any blood other than Caucasian", with a small allowance
for descendants of Pocahontas; the state registrar,
Walter Plecker, enforced it as a one-drop rule. Brazil sorts by appearance
instead. When its 1976 household survey let people name their own colour,
it recorded 136 terms, from morena to clara, yet 95 percent of answers fell
into seven. The census today offers five: branca, preta, parda, amarela and
indígena. Growing up in Nigeria, Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie has said, she thought of
herself as Igbo; identity there was ethnic and religious, and she became
Black in America. Compare [[paper-bag-test]], [[gannibal]] and
[[carolina-maria-de-jesus]].
