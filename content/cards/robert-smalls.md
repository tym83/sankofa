In May 1862 Robert Smalls, 23, was the enslaved pilot of the Planter, a Confederate transport in Charleston Harbor. On the night of 12 May its white crewmen went ashore. In the small hours, around 3 a.m. by one account, Smalls and the enslaved crew fired the boilers, picked up their families at another wharf and steamed past Fort Sumter, Smalls giving the right signals in the captain's hat. Sixteen or seventeen people, depending on the source, reached the Union blockade and freedom, and Smalls handed the ship to the Union Navy. He then piloted for the Navy, bought the Beaufort house of the man who had enslaved him, and served parts of five terms in the US House.
