In May 1862 Robert Smalls, 23, was the enslaved pilot of the Planter, a
Confederate transport in Charleston Harbor. On the night of 12 May its white
crewmen went ashore. Around 3 a.m. Smalls and the enslaved crew fired the
boilers, picked up their families at another wharf and steamed past Fort
Sumter and Fort Moultrie, Smalls giving the right signals in the captain's
hat. Sixteen people reached the Union blockade and freedom. He then
piloted for the Navy, bought the Beaufort house of the man who had enslaved
him, and served parts of five terms in the US House. Compare the
[[combahee-raid]] a year later and [[haitian-revolution|Haiti]].
