Emmett Till, fourteen, came from Chicago to visit family in the Mississippi
Delta. On 24 August 1955 Carolyn Bryant accused him of whistling at her in
her husband's store in Money. Four nights later Roy Bryant and J. W. Milam
took him from his great-uncle's house; his body was found in the Tallahatchie
River. In Chicago his mother, Mamie Till-Mobley, held
an open-casket funeral at Roberts Temple and let Jet photographer David
Jackson take pictures; Jet ran them in September. An all-white jury acquitted
both men in about an hour. In January 1956, safe from retrial, they sold
their confession to Look. Whether Carolyn Bryant later recanted is contested.
In 2022 a law in his name made lynching a federal hate crime. Compare
[[ida-b-wells]] and [[chicago-defender]].
