Granville Sharp was a clerk in the Ordnance Office at the Tower of London. In
1765 he took in Jonathan Strong, beaten and thrown into the street by his
master, and later stopped his shipment to Jamaica. In
1772 Sharp backed James Somerset's case, and Lord Mansfield ruled that no master
could force a slave out of England to be sold abroad. In 1783 [[equiano]]
brought him the [[zong]] killings, and Sharp pressed for murder charges. In
1787 he chaired the new Society for Effecting the Abolition of the Slave Trade
and championed the Province of Freedom, a settlement for Black Londoners in
Sierra Leone; by 1791 only 60 of its settlers survived. Off duty he played the
flageolet in family concerts on a barge on the Thames.
