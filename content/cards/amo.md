In 1729, at the University of Halle, a man born in Africa stood up to argue that Africans had the same claim as Europeans not to be enslaved in Europe. Anton Wilhelm Amo was born near Axim on the Gold Coast and reached the court of the dukes of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel as a small child, serving there as a "court Moor". His disputation on the rights of Moors in Europe is lost; only later summaries survive. In 1734 at Wittenberg he took his doctorate with De humanae mentis apatheia, and he went on to lecture at Halle and Jena. Facing racist hostility, he went home to Axim around 1747.
