Born near Axim on the Gold Coast, Amo was a small child when he reached the
court of the dukes of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel, where he served as a "court
Moor" and was baptised Anton Wilhelm in 1708. The dukes paid for his
schooling. At Halle in 1729 he defended a disputation on the rights of Moors
in Europe, arguing that Africans had the same claim as Europeans not to be
enslaved. Only later summaries of it survive. In 1734 at Wittenberg he took
his doctorate with De humanae mentis apatheia, which turned Descartes against
himself: the mind does not sense, only the body does. He lectured at Halle and
Jena, then, facing racist hostility, went home to Axim around 1747. Compare
[[juan-latino]] and [[equiano]].
