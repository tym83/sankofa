Slavery existed in Africa long before European ships arrived, and Paul
Lovejoy shows that the enslaved there were used extensively in
production. The Atlantic trade needed African
sellers, and states and merchant networks supplied them. Ouidah, the
second town of the kingdom of Dahomey, was the leading embarkation point
of the Slave Coast from the seventeenth to the nineteenth century. In the
Bight of Biafra, with no large state, the Aro merchants of Arochukwu drove
the trade's expansion through Igboland, the country [[equiano]] called
home. None of this lightens European guilt; it adds African
responsibility. In December 1999 Benin's president Mathieu Kérékou
convened a reconciliation conference in Cotonou, and Ghana's 2019 Year of
Return invited the diaspora home. Count the voyages in [[slavevoyages]];
see the currency in [[cowries]].
