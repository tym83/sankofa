In the early 1830s Mọmọlu Duwalu Bukẹlẹ of Jondu, in the Vai country of
western Liberia, created a way to write Vai. He told the missionary
S. W. Koelle, who printed it in 1849 and spelled him Doalu Bukara, how it
began: in a dream a tall, venerable white stranger brought him
a book and showed him that one sign meant i and another na, so together they
spelled ina, "come here". He woke and, with his brother and four cousins,
worked out a syllabary. By the late 1800s traders reported that most Vai
could read it, and it served for letters and accounts. In 2008 Unicode 5.1
gave it a block: ꔤ (i, U+A524), ꕯ (na, U+A56F), ꕪ (ka, U+A56A). Compare
[[liberia]], [[bamum]] and [[nsibidi]].
