One blues record in 1920 opened a whole market. The songwriter Perry Bradford persuaded OKeh to use Mamie Smith, a vaudeville singer and dancer, and on 10 August 1920 she recorded his "Crazy Blues" with her Jazz Hounds. NPR reports 10,000 copies sold in the first week and 75,000 within a month; bigger numbers, up to a million, are often repeated but unproven. The record opened the "race records" market. Next came Bessie Smith and the [[blues|blues]] boom, and a market for [[jazz|jazz]] on record. Compare [[big-mama-thornton|Willie Mae "Big Mama" Thornton]].
