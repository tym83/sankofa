Until 1920 no Black singer had recorded a blues. Earlier that year Sophie
Tucker, booked for an OKeh session, fell ill, and the songwriter Perry
Bradford persuaded the label to use Mamie Smith, a vaudeville singer and
dancer, instead. On 10 August 1920 she recorded his "Crazy Blues" with her
Jazz Hounds. NPR reports 10,000 copies sold in the first week and 75,000
within a month; bigger numbers, up to a million, are often repeated but
unproven. Bradford had sold the industry an idea: Black buyers wanted
records of their own. In 1921 OKeh launched a "race records" series. Next
came Bessie Smith and the [[blues]] boom, and a market for [[jazz]] on
record.
