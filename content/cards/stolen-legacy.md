In 1954 George G. M. James, a college teacher in Arkansas, published Stolen
Legacy. It said Greek philosophy was stolen Egyptian philosophy: Socrates learned
in Egypt, and Aristotle took his ideas from Egyptian texts in the Library of
Alexandria. The book spread for a reason. It was written when schools and
textbooks routinely denied that Africans had achieved anything, and it turned
that insult around. But the story is a myth. Aristotle died before the
Library was founded under the Ptolemies, and the "Egyptian mysteries" James
relied on come from Freemasonry and an 18th-century French novel, not from
ancient Egypt. Mary Lefkowitz took it apart in 1996. Real African learning
needs no theft: see [[timbuktu-manuscripts]] and [[amo]].
