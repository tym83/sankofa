One date sinks the story that Aristotle looted Egypt's wisdom: he died before the Library of Alexandria was founded under the Ptolemies. The story comes from Stolen Legacy, published in 1954 by George G. M. James, a college teacher in Arkansas. It said Greek philosophy was stolen Egyptian philosophy and that Aristotle took his ideas from Egyptian texts in the Library. Historians and classicists reject the thesis; Mary Lefkowitz took it apart in 1996. Real African learning needs no theft: see [[timbuktu-manuscripts|the Timbuktu manuscripts]], [[amo|Anton Wilhelm Amo]] and [[what-black-means|what "Black" means]].
