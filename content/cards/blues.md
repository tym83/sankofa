The blues took shape in the Deep South in the 1890s, above all in the
Mississippi Delta, out of [[work-songs]], hollers and [[spirituals]].
Around 1903 the bandleader W. C. Handy, waiting for a train at
Tutwiler, heard a man press a knife to his guitar strings and sing "Goin'
where the Southern cross' the Dog". Handy called it the weirdest music he
had ever heard, and built a career publishing such tunes. The first blues
stars on record were women: [[crazy-blues|Mamie Smith]] in 1920, then
Bessie Smith, whose 1923 Columbia debut came after years touring with Ma
Rainey, the "Mother of the Blues". The blues fed [[jazz]], [[gospel]] and
[[rock-n-roll]].
