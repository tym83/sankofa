Gerald "Jerry" Lawson grew up in Queens building ham radios and selling
walkie-talkies, self-taught in electronics. At Fairchild Camera and
Instrument in Silicon Valley he built a coin-operated game, Demolition
Derby, in his garage on Fairchild's new F8 microprocessor and tested it in a
pizza parlour in 1975. Fairchild put him in charge of its video products.
His team's Channel F (1976) was the first home console with interchangeable
cartridges, which Fairchild called Videocarts. The Atari 2600 soon eclipsed
it, but the cartridge model stayed. Lawson, one of the first Black engineers
in Silicon Valley and an early Homebrew Computer Club member, later founded
Video Soft. The game developers' association IGDA honoured him in March 2011,
a month before he died. Compare [[adlam]] and [[octavia-butler]].
