The first home console with interchangeable game cartridges came from a team led by Gerald "Jerry" Lawson. He grew up in Queens building ham radios and selling walkie-talkies, and became one of the first Black engineers in Silicon Valley and an early Homebrew Computer Club member. At Fairchild he built a coin-operated game, Demolition Derby, in his garage on Fairchild's F8 microprocessor and tested it in a pizza parlour. Fairchild put him in charge of its video products. His team's Channel F (1976) took cartridges Fairchild called Videocarts. The Atari 2600 soon eclipsed it. Lawson later founded Video Soft. Compare [[adlam|Adlam]] and [[octavia-butler|Octavia E. Butler]].
