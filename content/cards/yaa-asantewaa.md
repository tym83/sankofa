On 29 March 1900 Governor Frederick Hodgson held a durbar in Kumasi, capital of
Asante, whose king Prempeh had been exiled by the British. His wife recorded
that the Queen of Ejisu inspected his medals, said she admired his uniform,
and shook her hand. Then Hodgson asked the chiefs to hand over the Golden
Stool, which holds the soul of the Asante nation and on which no one sits.
Yaa Asantewaa, Queen Mother of Ejisu, led the rising that followed, and the
British were besieged in their fort. She was captured in 1901 and exiled to
the Seychelles, where she died on 17 October 1921. The British never got the
stool. Compare [[nanny-of-the-maroons]] and [[nzinga]].
