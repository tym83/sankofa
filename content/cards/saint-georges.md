Haydn's six Paris symphonies were ordered by an orchestra founded by a swordsman from Guadeloupe. Joseph Bologne, Chevalier de Saint-Georges, was a celebrated fencer and violinist. In 1772 he played his own concertos with the Concert des Amateurs and soon led it. When it folded in 1781 he founded the Concert de la Loge Olympique, a Masonic orchestra, and it was for this orchestra that Haydn wrote the six symphonies, composed in 1785 and 1786 and probably first heard in Paris in 1787. In 1792 the musician became colonel of a cavalry legion of men of colour. Compare [[general-dumas]] and [[bridgetower]].
