Born in Guadeloupe in 1745 to Nanon, an enslaved woman, and the planter
Georges Bologne, who acknowledged him, Joseph was raised in Paris as a
gentleman and became the most famous fencer in Europe. He was also a
virtuoso violinist: in 1772 he played his own concertos with the Concert des
Amateurs and soon led it. When it folded in 1781 he founded the Concert de la
Loge Olympique, a Masonic orchestra with over forty violins, and Haydn wrote
his six Paris symphonies for it; they premiered in 1786. In 1792 he became
colonel of a cavalry legion of men of colour, with [[general-dumas]] as his
second. Today he is often billed as "the Black Mozart", though he was eleven
years older. Compare [[bridgetower]].
