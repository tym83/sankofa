In 1621 a man listed as "Antonio, a Negro" arrived in Virginia on the James. He survived the 1622 Indian attack on the Bennett plantation, followed the Bennetts to the Eastern Shore with his wife Mary, won his freedom and became a landowner with 250 acres. Then the uncomfortable part. His own servant, John Casor, claimed his indenture was over. On 8 March 1655 the Northampton court ordered Casor returned to "his said master Anthony Johnson". It is often called America's first legal slave case; that label is contested. Compare [[partus-1662]] and [[aro-and-dahomey]].
