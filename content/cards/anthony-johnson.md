Antonio came to Jamestown on the James in 1621 and survived the 1622 Indian
attack on the Bennett plantation. With his wife Mary he followed the Bennetts
to the Eastern Shore, won freedom after 1635, took the name Anthony Johnson,
and by 1651 held 250 acres on the Pungoteague River. Then the uncomfortable
part: his servant John Casor claimed his indenture was over and went to a
white neighbour, Robert Parker. On 8 March 1655 the Northampton court ordered
Casor returned to "his said master Anthony Johnson", for life. It is often
called America's first legal slave case; that label is contested.
Free Black slaveholders existed later too: the South Carolina gin maker
William Ellison held 63 people in 1860. Compare [[partus-1662]] and
[[aro-and-dahomey]].
