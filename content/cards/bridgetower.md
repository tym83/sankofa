The violin sonata everyone knows as the "Kreutzer" was written for George
Bridgetower, a virtuoso of African descent and one of the most celebrated
violinists of his day. On 24 May 1803 he and Beethoven gave its first
performance in the Augarten in Vienna. Beethoven had revised the piece so late
that Bridgetower played from the composer's own manuscript. On that autograph,
now in the Beethoven-Haus in Bonn, Beethoven wrote a joking Italian dedication
to "il Mulatto Brischdauer gran pazzo e compositore mulattico", the mulatto
Bridgetower, great madman and mulatto composer. Then the
friendship broke, reportedly over a woman, and when the sonata was printed in
1805 it was dedicated to the French violinist Rodolphe Kreutzer, who never
played it. Compare [[saint-georges]], another Black virtuoso in Europe.
