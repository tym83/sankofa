The violin sonata everyone knows as the "Kreutzer", Op. 47, was written for George Bridgetower, a virtuoso of African descent. On 24 May 1803 he and Beethoven gave its first performance. On the autograph, now in the Beethoven-Haus in Bonn, Beethoven wrote a joking Italian dedication to "il Mulatto Brischdauer gran pazzo e compositore mulattico", the mulatto Bridgetower, great madman and mulatto composer. Then the friendship broke, reportedly over a woman, and when the sonata was printed in 1805 it was dedicated to the French violinist Rodolphe Kreutzer. Compare [[saint-georges|Joseph Bologne, Chevalier de Saint-Georges]], another Black virtuoso in Europe, and [[general-dumas|General Thomas-Alexandre Dumas]].
