Henrietta Lacks, a tobacco farmer from southern Virginia raising five
children, came to Johns Hopkins Hospital in Baltimore with cervical cancer. In
1951 doctors kept a sample of her tumour cells; no one asked her. She died that
year, aged 31, and was buried in an unmarked grave. Her cells did not die. The
HeLa line became the first immortalised human cell line, used in the polio
vaccine and more than 74,000 studies, while her family was left out. Rebecca
Skloot's 2010 book made the story public. In 2013 the NIH gave the family a say
over access to the HeLa genome, and in 2023 they settled a lawsuit against
Thermo Fisher Scientific on confidential terms. Her pair is [[tuskegee]].
