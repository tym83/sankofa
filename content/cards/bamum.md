A king designed a script for his own people: Ibrahim Njoya of Bamum, in western Cameroon. He began with roughly 500 signs for things and actions, a memory aid more than a script, and then simplified the pictorial signs step by step into a working system of writing. What happened next is contested. According to the Endangered Alphabets Project, the French administration that took over after the First World War destroyed his libraries and press and banned the teaching; other historians find no evidence of a ban. Compare [[vai-script]], [[nsibidi]], [[nko]] and [[adlam]].
