Thomas-Alexandre was born in Jérémie, Saint-Domingue, in 1762, the son of
Marie-Cessette, an enslaved woman, and the French nobleman Antoine Davy de la
Pailleterie, who pawned the boy before bringing him to France. He enlisted
under his mother's surname, and in 1792 the [[saint-georges|Chevalier de
Saint-Georges]] made him second in command of his Black Legion. Within a
year he was a general; he would command whole armies, including the Army of
the Alps. Sailing home from Egypt in 1799, his leaking ship put in at
Taranto, where he was held prisoner for two years. He came back broken and
died in 1806. His son turned that captivity into Edmond Dantès's. Compare
[[haitian-revolution]].
