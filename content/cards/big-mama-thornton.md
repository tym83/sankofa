In August 1952, in Los Angeles, the young songwriters Jerry Leiber and Mike
Stoller offered Willie Mae Thornton a twelve-bar blues called "Hound Dog".
She growled it as a woman throwing out a freeloading man. Released by
Houston's Peacock Records in 1953, it went to number one on the R&B chart
and stayed there seven weeks. By most accounts she received about $500.
In 1956 Elvis Presley's smoothed-out version brought him fame and a
fortune; in a 1974 interview she set his millions against her own zero.
Her own song "Ball and Chain" became a signature for Janis Joplin, her great admirer. Like
[[rosetta-tharpe]], she is a dependency [[rock-n-roll]] rarely credited.
See [[blues]].
