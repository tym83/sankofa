"Hound Dog" was a number-one hit three years before Elvis Presley touched it. In August 1952 the young songwriters Jerry Leiber and Mike Stoller offered the song to Willie Mae "Big Mama" Thornton (1926–1984). Released by Houston's Peacock Records in 1953, her recording went to number one on the R&B chart; how many copies it sold is disputed. In 1956 Presley's version brought him fame and a fortune. In a 1974 interview she set his millions against her own zero. Her own song "Ball and Chain" became a signature for Janis Joplin. Like [[rosetta-tharpe]], she is a dependency [[rock-n-roll]] rarely credited. See [[blues]].
