Enslaved people made the spirituals from Bible stories, hymns and the
call-and-response of [[work-songs]], and the songs could mean two things at
once. Frederick Douglass recalled singing "O Canaan, sweet Canaan, I am
bound for the land of Canaan" while planning an escape: "We meant to reach
the north—and the north was our Canaan." On 6 October 1871 nine students
left Fisk University, a freedmen's school about to go under, carrying the
whole treasury for travel. They raised about $40,000 on their first tour;
in May 1873 they sang for Queen Victoria and Gladstone in England. Later
claims of songs as escape maps are another matter, see
[[underground-railroad-myths]]. Spirituals fed the [[blues]] and
[[gospel]].
