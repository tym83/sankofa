On 6 October 1871 nine students left Fisk University in Nashville, a freedmen's school about to go under, carrying the whole treasury for travel. They were going on tour to save it, and their music would take them all the way to Queen Victoria. They raised tens of thousands of dollars on that first tour, though the figures given vary. What they sang were spirituals, made by enslaved people from Bible stories, hymns and call-and-response. Later claims of songs as escape maps are another matter, see [[underground-railroad-myths]].
