On 5 July 1787 the new Committee for the Abolition of the Slave Trade
commissioned a seal: a kneeling Black man in chains asking "Am I Not a
Man and a Brother?" The potter Josiah Wedgwood, a leading abolitionist,
had it modelled in jasperware that year, probably by William Hackwood,
and gave the cameos away. Thomas Clarkson asked
for as many as could be spared for his speaking tour; in 1788 Wedgwood
sent a batch to Benjamin Franklin in Philadelphia. People set them into
snuffbox lids, bracelets and hairpins. In America the poet Elizabeth
Margaret Chandler built a poem around "Am I Not a Woman and a Sister?",
and by 1836 the kneeling woman was the emblem of women's antislavery
societies. Its twin: the [[brookes]] print; see also [[equiano]].
