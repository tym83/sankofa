Publishers wanted Kossola's story in standard English; Zora Neale Hurston refused, and the book waited until 2018. She grew up in Eatonville, Florida, one of the first towns in the United States incorporated by Black people, where her father became mayor. She studied anthropology at Barnard under Franz Boas and went south to collect folklore. In Mobile in 1927 she sat with Kossola, called Cudjo Lewis, a survivor of the [[clotilda]], and took down his story in his own speech: Barracoon. Their Eyes Were Watching God (1937) she wrote in seven weeks. She died in a county welfare home in Fort Pierce in 1960 and lay in an unmarked grave until Alice Walker located it in 1973 and placed a stone: "A Genius of the South".
