Hurston grew up in Eatonville, Florida, one of the first towns in the United
States incorporated by Black people, where her father became mayor. She
studied anthropology at Barnard under Franz Boas and went south to collect
folklore. In 1927 and 1928 she sat in Mobile
with Kossola, called Cudjo Lewis, a survivor of the [[clotilda]], and took down
his story in his own speech. Publishers wanted it rewritten in standard
English; she refused, and Barracoon appeared only in 2018. Their Eyes Were Watching God
(1937) she wrote in seven weeks. She died in a county welfare
home in Fort Pierce in 1960 and lay in an unmarked grave until Alice Walker
located the spot in 1973 and placed a stone: "A Genius of the South".
