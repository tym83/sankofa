In 1788 British abolitionists commissioned a drawing of how people were
packed aboard the Liverpool slave ship Brooks, also spelled Brookes. The
first version came from William Elford and the Plymouth abolition
society, its date disputed between late 1788 and March 1789: a single
plan of bodies laid in rows inside a hull. In London James Phillips
printed it as Description of a Slave Ship. Between March and July 1789
more than 10,000 copies appeared in Plymouth, Philadelphia and London
versions; on 28 July the London committee ordered 1,700 with a copper
plate and 7,000 with woodcuts. Evidence gathered by the Commons became
something anyone could see. Its partner in the campaign was the
[[wedgwood-medallion]]; the reality behind it is in [[zong]] and
[[slavevoyages]].
