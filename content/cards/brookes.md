A single plan of bodies laid in rows inside a hull: that was the picture abolitionists chose to show Britain. In 1788 they commissioned a drawing of how people were packed aboard the Liverpool slave ship Brooks, also spelled Brookes. The first version came out of Plymouth; when exactly is disputed, late 1788 or March 1789. In London James Phillips printed it in 1789 as Description of a Slave Ship, and a cargo list became an image nobody could unsee. Its partner in the campaign was [[wedgwood-medallion|the Wedgwood medallion "Am I Not a Man and a Brother?"]]; the reality behind it is in [[zong|the Zong massacre]] and [[slavevoyages|SlaveVoyages]].
