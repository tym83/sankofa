Sunjata Keita founded the Mali Empire in the early thirteenth century,
after defeating Sumaoro Kanté, king of Sosso. His story lives as
performance: the jeli, or griot, trained from childhood, sings it to the
balafon. In the epic the jeli Balla Foureke (Fasséké) advises the king and
keeps royal memory, as jeliw still do. The Sosso-Bala, a twenty-slat
balafon said to have been Sumaoro's own, is kept in a mud hut at
Nyagassola in Guinea; only its guardian, the Balatigui, patriarch of the
Dökala family of Kouyaté griots, may play it. Tradition says Sunjata's assembly then proclaimed the
Manden Charter at Kurukan Fuga, still passed down orally by Malinke clans
and listed by UNESCO in 2009. Sankofa's Griot operator is named for these
keepers of memory. See [[mansa-musa]], [[talking-drums]].
