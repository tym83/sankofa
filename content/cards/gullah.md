Amelia Dawley of Georgia sang the linguist Lorenzo Dow Turner a song whose
words she could not translate. It was Mende. Decades later researchers
found a woman in the village of Senehun Ngola, Sierra Leone, who knew the
same funeral song, and in 1997 Dawley's family went there; the film *The
Language You Cry In* (1998) tells the story. The song survived in Gullah
country: on the Sea Islands of the Carolinas and Georgia, descendants of
enslaved West and Central Africans made Gullah, a creole. Many called it
broken English until Turner's *Africanisms in the Gullah Dialect* (1949).
