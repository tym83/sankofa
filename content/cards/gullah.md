Gullah, also called Geechee, is a creole: a new language that grew from English and many African languages. Most of its vocabulary is English, but its grammar does not simply follow English, and it keeps words of African origin. Lorenzo Dow Turner documented thousands of African personal names in his historical study. It developed among enslaved West and Central Africans and their descendants on the rice, Sea Island cotton and indigo plantations of coastal South Carolina and Georgia, where isolated islands let them keep many African traditions.

Amelia Dawley of Georgia sang the linguist Lorenzo Dow Turner a song whose words she could not translate. It was Mende. Decades later researchers found a woman in the village of Senehun Ngola, Sierra Leone, who knew the same funeral song, and in 1997 Dawley's family went there; the film The Language You Cry In (1998) tells the story. The song survived in Gullah country, on the Sea Islands of the Carolinas and Georgia. Many called it broken English until Turner's Africanisms in the Gullah Dialect (1949), which traced thousands of its words and names to African languages.
