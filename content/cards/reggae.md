Reggae grew in West Kingston, among people pushed to the margins of
Jamaican life. Ska slowed into rocksteady and then into reggae, taking in
soul and rhythm and blues from the United States. Its engine was the sound
system: from the late 1940s, mobile discos with a selector at the records
and a "deejay" who toasted over the instrumental parts. In 1968 the Maytals
recorded "Do the Reggay" for producer Leslie Kong, the first song to use the
name. Toots Hibbert said the word came to him in Trench Town from
"streggay", slang for a person who does not dress well. The sound-system
habit crossed to the Bronx with the Campbell siblings, see
[[cindy-campbell]] and [[hip-hop]]. In 2018 UNESCO inscribed reggae music of
Jamaica on its list of intangible heritage.
