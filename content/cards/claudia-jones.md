Born in Port of Spain in 1915 and raised in Harlem, Claudia Jones became a
Communist Party activist in New York. She was jailed four times, and amid
McCarthyism the United States deported her to Britain in 1955. In March 1958
she founded the West Indian Gazette in Brixton. That summer serious race
riots broke out in Notting Hill and Nottingham. Her answer was joy: on 30
January 1959 she staged a Caribbean carnival at St Pancras Town Hall, partly
televised. Such indoor carnivals fed the outdoor Notting Hill Carnival, first
held in 1966, two years after her death. She is buried in Highgate, just to
the left of Karl Marx. Back in Trinidad, carnival had found its own new
instrument, the [[steelpan]]; her paper's American elder was the
[[chicago-defender]].
