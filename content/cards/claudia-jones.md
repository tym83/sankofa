Claudia Jones answered race riots with a carnival. Born in Trinidad in 1915 and raised in Harlem, she became a Communist Party activist in New York; she was jailed four times, and amid McCarthyism the United States deported her to Britain in 1955. In March 1958 she founded the West Indian Gazette in Brixton. That summer race riots broke out in Notting Hill and Nottingham. In January 1959 she staged a Caribbean carnival at St Pancras Town Hall, partly televised. Such indoor carnivals fed the outdoor Notting Hill Carnival, first held in 1966, two years after her death. She is buried in Highgate, next to Karl Marx. Back in Trinidad, carnival had its new instrument, the [[steelpan|steelpan]]; her paper's American elder was the [[chicago-defender|Chicago Defender]].
