In February 2001 Henry Louis Gates Jr. bought an unpublished novel, handwritten probably in
the 1850s, at Swann Galleries in New York for $8,500, the only bidder.
It came from the estate of the Howard University librarian Dorothy Porter
Wesley, and its title page named the author as "Hannah Crafts, a fugitive slave
recently escaped from North Carolina".
Published in 2002 as *The Bondwoman's Narrative*, it became a bestseller and
may be the first novel by a Black woman to show slavery from the inside. Gates
gave the manuscript to Yale's Beinecke Library. Who was Crafts? In 2013 Gregg
Hecimovich named Hannah Bond, enslaved by the North Carolina planter John Hill
Wheeler, after a decade in wills, diaries and records. The case is
widely accepted, though still argued. Compare [[jacobs-garret]].
