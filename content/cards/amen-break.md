In 1969 the Winstons, an integrated soul band whose leader Richard L.
Spencer wrote and sang "Color Him Father", had a hit that won a Grammy. The
B-side, the instrumental "Amen, Brother", was cut in a hurry; drummer
Gregory C. Coleman later said he probably rushed the tempo. Its four-bar
break was looped in [[hip-hop]], then sped up and chopped into jungle and
drum and bass, on air on stations like [[rinse-fm]]. Neither man received
sample royalties; Spencer learned how famous it was only in 1996. Coleman
died in 2006, reportedly homeless and broke. In 2015 a UK DJ, Martyn
Webster, crowdfunded £24,000 for Spencer, who died in 2020. Container images
are built the same way, layer on layer over a base nobody credits. Compare
[[funk]] drummers and [[planet-rock]].
