Seized as a boy of about seven and held in Constantinople, he was brought to
Peter the Great around 1705 and baptised at Vilna in 1707 with the tsar as
godfather. He insisted on being called Abram, not Pyotr. Peter sent him to
France to study military engineering; back home he served as an engineer,
fell into disgrace after Peter's death and rose again under Elizabeth to
general-in-chief. He died in 1781. Where he was born is disputed: Ethiopia in
the old account, the Logone sultanate near Lake Chad in Dieudonné
Gnammankou's. His great-grandson Alexander Pushkin began a novel about him in
1827. Compare [[ira-aldridge]] and [[oliver-golden]], and ask
[[what-black-means]] in Russia.
