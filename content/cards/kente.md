Asante tradition says two men of Bonwire learned to weave kente by watching a spider spin its web. The cloth is woven in long narrow strips on horizontal looms, then sewn edge to edge into a large cloth. Ewe weavers to the east have their own tradition. Kente designs carry names, taken from proverbs, sayings or events, so a cloth speaks as well as dazzles. Across the Atlantic, kente stoles became a fixture of Black graduations in the United States.
