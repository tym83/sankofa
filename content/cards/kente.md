Kente is woven in long narrow strips on horizontal looms, then sewn edge
to edge into a large cloth. Asante tradition says two men of Bonwire,
near Kumasi, learned the craft by watching a spider spin its web, and
Bonwire is still called the capital of kente; Ewe weavers to the east
have their own tradition. Each
design is named by the weaver who created it, after proverbs, sayings or
events. The North Carolina Museum of Art holds one in the pattern Darkoro
Yesre, "Certainly one day you will laugh". In 2024 UNESCO listed the
craft as intangible heritage of humanity. Across the Atlantic, kente
stoles became a fixture of Black graduations in the United States. Kin:
[[adinkra]], stamped not woven, and [[benin-bronzes]].
