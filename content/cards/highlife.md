In the Gold Coast brass bands, sailors' palm-wine guitar and ballroom
orchestras met. Around 1919 the guitarist Jacob Sam and his Kumasi Trio
composed "Yaa Amponsah", often heard as highlife's prototype; by the 1920s
orchestras played for the elite in Accra's clubs, and onlookers outside
named it, the story goes. After the war E. T. Mensah took it out of the
ballroom with a small swing-style band, the Tempos. When Louis Armstrong
came to Accra in May 1956, Mensah's "All For You" greeted him, and Armstrong heard in it
a Creole tune from New Orleans. Tempos
tours lit up Nigeria: Bobby Benson's brass sound in Lagos inspired a young
Fela, the road to [[afrobeat]], and Victor Olaiya became a star. Compare [[jazz]] and the
palm wine of [[tutuola]].
