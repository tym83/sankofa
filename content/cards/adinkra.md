Adinkra cloth is Asante: cotton woven in strips, sewn together and
stamped in rows of signs, each with a name and a message. Before colonial
rule it was mainly worn at funerals; Ntonso, near Kumasi, still stamps it. In 1817 Thomas Bowdich, on a
mission to the Asante capital, acquired a stamped adinkra cloth that
reached the British Museum in 1818. One sign, a bird looking back or a
stylised heart, is Sankofa, an Akan precept that literally means "go back
and get it": heritage as a tool to build the future. A heart of
tacks on a colonial-era coffin lid in New York's African Burial
Ground was hailed as Sankofa, a reading now contested. Sankofa takes its
name from the sign. Compare [[kente]] and [[nsibidi]].
