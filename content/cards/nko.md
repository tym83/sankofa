Around 1949 Souleymane Kanté, from Kankan in Guinea, was living in
Bingerville in Côte d'Ivoire when, the story goes, he read that Africans
lacked writing of their own. He answered by inventing one: N'Ko, for the
Manding languages. Its users mark 14 April 1949 as
Alphabet Day. The exact provocation is contested; the result is not. Back in
Kankan and then from the Gambia to Nigeria, a literacy movement spread it
without state support, through textbooks, a dictionary, newspapers and a
Qur'an translation. With Unicode 5.0 it reached computers and phones. Africa had written
before, from [[nsibidi]] to the [[vai-script]] and [[bamum]]; a later
script born the same way is [[adlam]]. On writing your own language, see
[[ngugi]].
