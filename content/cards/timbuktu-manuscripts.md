Timbuktu's families kept books for centuries: Qur'anic scholarship,
mathematics, astronomy, in household libraries and later in the state Ahmed Baba
Institute. When armed groups took the city on 1 April 2012, Abdel Kader
Haidara, keeper of the Mamma Haidara library and head of the owners'
association SAVAMA-DCI, organised an escape. Volunteers packed manuscripts
into trunks by flashlight, two trunks per car to avoid attention;
some left on donkey carts or in canoes on the Niger. Hamburg researchers
count about 2,400 trunks reaching Bamako, National Geographic about 350,000
manuscripts from 45 libraries. Before fleeing in early 2013 the militants
burned about 4,200 manuscripts at the Ahmed Baba Institute. Kant and Hegel
imagined an Africa without books; compare [[nsibidi]] and [[adinkra]],
and the Mali of [[mansa-musa]].
