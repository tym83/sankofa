In March 1951 Ike Turner's Kings of Rhythm cut "Rocket 88" at Sam
Phillips's studio in Memphis, a song about an Oldsmobile; it was issued
under the singer Jackie Brenston's name. It is often called the first rock
and roll record, though that is debated. That July in Cleveland the DJ
Alan Freed began playing rhythm and blues and calling it rock and roll.
Then came Fats Domino from New Orleans, Chuck Berry's "Maybellene" (1955)
and Little Richard's "Tutti Frutti". Labels aimed separate versions at
white buyers: in 1956 Pat Boone's cover of "Tutti Frutti" outsold the
original. Under it all lay the [[blues]], [[gospel]],
[[rosetta-tharpe]]'s guitar and [[big-mama-thornton]]'s "Hound Dog".
