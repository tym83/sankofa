In 1900 the Exposition Universelle in Paris had a Negro Exhibit in the
American section, put together by Thomas J. Calloway, W. E. B. Du Bois,
Booker T. Washington and Daniel Murray of the Library of Congress. Its job
was to show the economic and social progress of African Americans since
emancipation. Du Bois and his students at Atlanta University answered with
charts in English and French: Georgia counties coloured by Black
population, land and property, a line showing slavery shrinking from 1790
to 1870, one even setting the age profile of Black Georgians against France's. They
were drawn by hand, and survive in the Library of Congress's Daniel Murray
collection. Compare [[hurston]] and [[freedoms-journal]].
