In 1835 Harriet Jacobs, escaping Dr. James Norcom and his years of sexual
threats, hid in a crawlspace over a shed at her grandmother's house in Edenton,
North Carolina. "The garret was only nine feet long, and seven wide.
The highest part was three feet high," she wrote; there was no light, no air.
She stayed nearly seven years. With a gimlet her uncle had left behind she
bored a hole an inch square and watched her two children in the street. In
1842 she escaped by boat to the North. In 1861 she published *Incidents in the
Life of a Slave Girl* under the name Linda Brent. For over a century her
authorship was uncertain; Jean Fagan Yellin's 1981 article used her letters to establish it. Compare [[douglass-covey]].
