Between 1936 and 1938 the Federal Writers' Project, a New Deal program, sent
writers to interview people born into slavery. The
Library of Congress now holds more than 2,300 of their first-person accounts
and 500 photographs, online as *Born in Slavery*. Read them with care. Most
interviewers were white, some from former slaveholding families, and they were
told to capture "dialect", which often came out as stereotype. When the
historian Paul D. Escott compared, 72 percent of those questioned by
white interviewers called their food good, against 46 percent of those
questioned by Black interviewers. It also holds voices:
recordings of 22 formerly enslaved people made between 1932 and 1975. Several
sing songs learned in slavery (see [[spirituals]], [[work-songs]]), and five
were recorded in [[gullah]] country by Lorenzo Dow Turner.
