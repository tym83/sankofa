Ida B. Wells co-owned the Memphis *Free Speech*. After her friend Thomas Moss and
two partners in a Black grocery were lynched in 1892, she wrote in May:
"Nobody in this section of the country believes the old thread-bare lie that
Negro men rape white women." She was in New York when Memphis businessmen met
to threaten lynching; her partner fled, creditors sold the press, and she could
not go home. She answered with research. *Southern Horrors* (1892) and *A Red
Record* (1895) built their case on lynchings reported by white newspapers,
above all the *Chicago Tribune*'s annual tallies: "Out of their own mouths
shall the murderers be condemned." In 1893 and 1894 she lectured across
Britain, and a London Anti-Lynching Committee followed. Her line runs from
[[freedoms-journal]] to [[emmett-till]].
