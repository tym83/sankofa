Ida B. Wells proved lynching was terror by counting the dead in white newspapers' own reports. She co-owned the Memphis Free Speech. After her friend Thomas Moss and two partners in a Black grocery were lynched in 1892, she wrote in May: "Nobody in this section of the country believes the old thread-bare lie that Negro men rape white women." Threats forced her out of Memphis and she could not go home; by her own account creditors sold the press while she was in New York, though accounts differ. Southern Horrors (1892) and A Red Record (1895) built their case on lynchings reported by white newspapers, above all the Chicago Tribune's annual tallies: "Out of their own mouths shall the murderers be condemned." Her line runs from [[freedoms-journal|Freedom's Journal]] to [[emmett-till|Emmett Till]].
