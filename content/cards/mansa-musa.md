In 1324 Musa, ruler of Mali, stopped in Cairo on his way to Mecca. The
writer al-Umari, who came to Cairo a few years later, collected the
stories: Musa refused at first to meet the sultan, saying he had come for
the pilgrimage and nothing else, and he gave a load of gold to every emir
and office-holder. The gold flooded the market. A mithqal of gold that had
not gone below 25 dirhams fell to 22 or less, and al-Umari says it
had stayed there for about twelve years. Half a century later a Majorcan
mapmaker painted "Musse Melly" on the Catalan Atlas, crowned, holding a
gold disc, the richest king of the region. Mali's founding hero is
[[sunjata]]; its scholarship lives on in the [[timbuktu-manuscripts]].
