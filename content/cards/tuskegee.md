In 1932, in Macon County, Alabama, the US Public Health Service enrolled 600
Black men, 399 with syphilis and 201 without, to record how the disease ran
its course. The community took it for a special government health programme. By
1943 penicillin was the treatment of choice; the men were not offered it. In
1966 Peter Buxtun, a Public Health Service employee in San Francisco, wrote to
the CDC objecting, and was rebuffed for years. In 1972 he gave documents to
the Associated Press; Jean Heller's story ran on 25 July and the study ended
that year. A 1974 settlement paid survivors and heirs; in 1997 President
Clinton apologised. The last participant died in 2004. Its pair is
[[henrietta-lacks]].
