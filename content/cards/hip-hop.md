Hip-hop began with two copies of the same record. Clive Campbell, born in Kingston, Jamaica, and known as DJ Kool Herc, brought Jamaica's sound-system culture to the Bronx, his father's big speakers powering the parties. His trick was the merry-go-round: two copies of one funk or soul record on two turntables, switching between them so the drum break ran as long as he liked. The party usually dated as the start is [[cindy-campbell]]'s, in August 1973. Grandmaster Flash and Afrika Bambaataa built on it. The record that took rap to a mass market, "Rapper's Delight" (1979), rode Chic's "Good Times" bass line and ended in shared writing credits. Roots: [[reggae]], [[funk]]; heir: the [[amen-break]].
