Clive Campbell, a Kingston-born teenager known as DJ Kool Herc, brought
Jamaica's sound-system culture to the West Bronx: his father's big speakers
and a friend shouting rhymes over the records. His trick was the
"merry-go-round": two copies of the same funk or soul record on two
turntables, switching between them so the drum break dancers wanted ran
as long as he liked. The party usually dated as the start is
[[cindy-campbell]]'s, in August 1973. Grandmaster Flash and Afrika
Bambaataa built on it; Grand Wizzard Theodore, twelve, found the
scratch in 1975 when his mother told him to turn it down. The record that took rap
to a mass market, "Rapper's Delight" (1979), rode Chic's "Good Times" bass line and ended in
shared writing credits. Roots: [[reggae]], [[funk]]; heir: the
[[amen-break]].
