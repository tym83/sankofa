In the Recôncavo of Bahia, the samba de roda grew from the dances of
enslaved Africans; UNESCO proclaimed it heritage in 2005. Migrants carried it
south to Rio, where Bahian "aunts" led the Black neighbourhood around Praça
Onze, called Little Africa. The best known, Tia Ciata, born in Santo Amaro in
1854 and initiated in [[candomble]], held parties with choro in the front
room, samba in the yard and a candomblé ceremony to close. There, in 1916, a
song took shape that Donga registered at the National Library on 27
November: Pelo Telefone, the hit of the 1917 carnival. Other regulars said
the tune was collective. Compare [[capoeira]] and [[jazz]].
