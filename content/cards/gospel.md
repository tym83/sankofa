Thomas A. Dorsey came to Chicago from Georgia in 1916 and made his name in
the [[blues]]: as "Georgia Tom" he led Ma Rainey's band and had a bawdy hit
with Tampa Red. He began setting sacred words to blues patterns, and many
pastors called it the devil's music. In August 1932 his wife Nettie and
their son died in childbirth; in his grief he wrote "Take My Hand, Precious
Lord". In 1933 he co-founded a national convention of gospel choirs, and
later teamed with Mahalia Jackson, who had arrived from New Orleans in
1927. In 1939 a Chicago pastor, Clarence Cobbs, bought one of the earliest
Hammond organs, an instrument that could shout. Gospel singing and
guitar, as in [[rosetta-tharpe|Rosetta Tharpe]], fed rock and roll, soul
and [[funk]].
