The story does not start with chains. It starts with a king on pilgrimage. [[mansa-musa|Mansa Musa]] of Mali travelled to Mecca, and on a map drawn in Mallorca around 1375 he sits enthroned, holding gold up to the world.

Along the Niger bend, scholars copied books. When jihadists held Timbuktu in 2012, librarians carried hundreds of thousands of [[timbuktu-manuscripts|manuscripts]] out in trunks to keep them alive. Memory was also kept without paper: griots still sing the founding of Mali in [[sunjata|the epic of Sunjata]].

Africa engineered its own ways to send and store meaning. [[talking-drums|Talking drums]] carry the tones of Yoruba speech, so a drum can say what a voice says. Akan cloth carries [[adinkra|adinkra]] signs, among them Sankofa, "go back and get it", which gives this distribution its name. The patterns of [[kente|kente cloth]] are traditionally named, often with proverbs or historical associations, so a cloth can be read as well as worn. Compare the signs of [[nsibidi|Nsibidi]].

In Benin City a royal guild cast the plaques we call the [[benin-bronzes|Benin Bronzes]]; some of them show Portuguese traders. And from the Maldives came a small shell. [[cowries|Cowrie shells]] were money across West Africa, and they also became the price of human beings. That is where the next chapter begins.
