This is the Book of Voices: the story told by the people themselves. Before [[phillis-wheatley]] could print her poems in 1773, eighteen Boston men had to sign that an enslaved girl really wrote them. In North Carolina, [[omar-ibn-said]] wrote his life in Arabic. In New York, [[freedoms-journal]] opened with "We wish to plead our own cause."

Your copy of this chapter has one of two voices. In one, [[douglass-covey]]: a sixteen-year-old fights the man hired to break him and is never whipped by him again. In the other, [[jacobs-garret]]: a mother lies for nearly seven years in a crawlspace, watching her children through a hole. To read both, find someone who holds the other copy.

Some voices were not believed, or were rewritten. The famous refrain of [[sojourner-truth]] is not in the 1851 report of her speech. The handwritten novel of [[hannah-crafts]] waited until 2001 to be found.

Where writing was forbidden, people wrote on what they made. [[dave-the-potter]] carved poems into his jars. A mother packed [[ashleys-sack]] for a daughter who was being sold. A Georgia family kept a song in [[gullah]] country that a village in Sierra Leone still sang.

And they sang. [[work-songs]] timed the work, [[spirituals]] carried hope and sometimes code, and the [[banjo]] came from West African lutes. [[ida-b-wells]] counted the lynched to prove it was terror. In the 1930s, the [[wpa-narratives]] recorded more than 2,300 people born into slavery.
