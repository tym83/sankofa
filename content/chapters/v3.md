This is the Book of Voices: the story told by the people themselves. When [[phillis-wheatley|Phillis Wheatley]], an enslaved poet, printed her poems in 1773, eighteen Boston men signed a published attestation that she was their author. In North Carolina, [[omar-ibn-said|Omar ibn Said]] wrote his life in Arabic. In New York, [[freedoms-journal|Freedom's Journal]] opened with "We wish to plead our own cause."

Your copy of this chapter has one of two voices. In one, [[douglass-covey|Frederick Douglass fights Edward Covey]] and is never whipped by him again. In the other, [[jacobs-garret|Harriet Jacobs lies for nearly seven years in a crawlspace]]. To read both, find someone who holds the other copy.

Some voices were not heard as they spoke. The famous refrain of [[sojourner-truth|Sojourner Truth]], "Ain't I a woman?", is not in the 1851 report of her speech. Read [[hannah-crafts|The Bondwoman's Narrative]] beside her.

People also wrote on what they made. [[dave-the-potter|Dave the Potter]] carved poems into his jars. According to a family account embroidered by Ruth Middleton in 1921, Rose packed [[ashleys-sack|the sack now known as Ashley's Sack]] for her daughter before they were separated by sale. A Georgia family in [[gullah|Gullah Geechee]] country kept a song also known in Sierra Leone.

And they sang. Listen to [[work-songs|work songs, field hollers]] and [[spirituals|spirituals]]. The [[banjo|banjo]] came from West African lutes. [[ida-b-wells|Ida B. Wells]] counted the lynched to prove it was terror, turning numbers into testimony. Then turn to [[wpa-narratives|the WPA slave narratives collected in Born in Slavery]].
