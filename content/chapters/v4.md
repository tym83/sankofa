What does Black mean? It depends on where you stand. Asked to name their own colour, Brazilians in 1976 gave 136 answers, and a Nigerian novelist says she became Black only when she landed in America. Start with [[what-black-means]].

The diaspora runs further than most maps show. [[gannibal]], a kidnapped African boy, became Peter the Great's godson, a general, and the great-grandfather of Pushkin.

In Salvador, according to their own traditions, African priestesses founded Casa Branca and related religious lineages, institutions of [[candomble]] that survive today. [[capoeira]] was a crime in the 1890 penal code and is now heritage of humanity. In Rio, "Pelo Telefone", associated with the gatherings of Tia Ciata, was registered in 1916 and became the breakthrough [[samba]] hit of 1917. Several participants disputed Donga's authorship and claimed it as a collective creation, and scholars document earlier sambas still. In a São Paulo favela, [[carolina-maria-de-jesus]] wrote a diary on paper from the trash.

Freedom on paper was no guarantee. In 1930 a League of Nations inquiry found the government of [[liberia]] shipping its own people as forced labour. And Africa kept inventing writing. A Vai man dreamed that a stranger taught him to write, and the [[vai-script]] was born. In Cameroon, King Njoya invented [[bamum]] and refined it into a syllabary.

In Trinidad, young Port of Spain musicians, working amid restrictions on percussion, turned oil drums into an instrument: the [[steelpan]].
