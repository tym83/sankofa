"Black" does not mean the same thing everywhere. Asked to name their own colour, Brazilians in 1976 gave 136 answers; a Nigerian novelist says she became Black only when she landed in America. [[what-black-means]] is the map for this chapter.

Start in Russia. [[gannibal]], a kidnapped African boy, became Peter the Great's godson, a general, and the great-grandfather of Pushkin.

In Brazil, people built their own Black world. In Salvador, African women founded the [[candomble]] temples that still stand. [[capoeira]] was a crime in the 1890 penal code and is now heritage of humanity. In a Bahian woman's backyard in Rio, the first registered [[samba]] was made, and half the party claimed to have written it. In a São Paulo favela, [[carolina-maria-de-jesus]] wrote a diary on paper from the trash, and it sold out in three days.

Return was not simple either. [[liberia]] was founded by formerly enslaved Americans, and in 1930 a League of Nations inquiry found its government shipping its own people as forced labour. In the same country, a Vai man dreamed that a stranger taught him to write, and the [[vai-script]] was born. In Cameroon, King Njoya invented [[bamum]] and refined it into a syllabary.

In Trinidad, drums and then bamboo were banned, so young men in Port of Spain made the [[steelpan]] out of oil drums.
