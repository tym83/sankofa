Rock and roll was built on Black music. [[rosetta-tharpe|Sister Rosetta Tharpe]] played gospel guitar years before rock and roll had a name. In 1949 Billboard renamed its chart [[rhythm-and-blues|rhythm and blues]], a phrase already in its pages. [[big-mama-thornton|Willie Mae "Big Mama" Thornton]]'s "Hound Dog" was a number one three years before Elvis's, and she later set his millions against her own zero. Then came [[rock-n-roll|rock and roll]], and later [[funk|funk]], whose drum breaks would build hip-hop.

The century also kept terrible records. In 1955 [[emmett-till|Emmett Till]]'s mother kept his coffin open. For forty years the US Public Health Service watched Black men with syphilis go untreated in [[tuskegee|the Tuskegee study]]. Cells taken from [[henrietta-lacks|Henrietta Lacks]] in 1951, when she was 30 and without her knowledge, are still dividing today.

Writers took their stories back. [[achebe|Chinua Achebe]] told an Igbo village from the inside in Things Fall Apart. In The Palm-Wine Drinkard, [[tutuola|Amos Tutuola]] wrote a Yoruba journey in his own English. [[ngugi|Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o]] wrote a novel in Gikuyu on prison toilet paper. In Guinea, a man from Kankan answered the claim that Africans had no writing with an alphabet of his own, [[nko|N'Ko]].

Music crossed every border. Listen to [[afrobeat|Afrobeat]]. [[reggae|Reggae]], by Toots Hibbert's own account, got its name in Trench Town. In London, [[claudia-jones|Claudia Jones]] answered the 1958 riots with a carnival. [[radio-haiti|Radio Haïti-Inter]] gave the news in Creole and paid for it.

The community also argued with itself: about [[paper-bag-test|the paper bag test]] and the preference for lighter skin, and about [[stolen-legacy|Stolen Legacy]], a book that claimed Greek philosophy was stolen from Egypt.
