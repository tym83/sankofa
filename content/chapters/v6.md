Rock and roll has undeclared dependencies. [[rosetta-tharpe]] played gospel guitar years before rock and roll had a name. In 1949 Billboard renamed its chart [[rhythm-and-blues]], a phrase already in its pages. [[big-mama-thornton]]'s "Hound Dog" was a number one three years before Elvis's; by most accounts she was paid about $500, though that figure is contested. Then came [[rock-n-roll]], and later [[funk]], whose drum breaks would build hip-hop.

The century also kept terrible records. In 1955 [[emmett-till]]'s mother kept his coffin open. For forty years the US Public Health Service watched Black men with syphilis go untreated at [[tuskegee]]. Cells taken from [[henrietta-lacks]] in 1951, when she was 30 and without her knowledge, are still dividing today.

Writers took their stories back. [[achebe]] told an Igbo village from the inside in Things Fall Apart. [[tutuola]] wrote a Yoruba journey in his own English. [[ngugi]] wrote a novel in Gikuyu on prison toilet paper. Compare the script [[nko]].

Music crossed every border. Listen to [[afrobeat]]. [[reggae]], by Toots Hibbert's own account, got its name in Trench Town. In London, [[claudia-jones]] answered the 1958 riots with a carnival. [[radio-haiti]] gave the news in Creole and paid for it.

Two cards turn the questions inward: [[paper-bag-test]] and [[stolen-legacy]].
