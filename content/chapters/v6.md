Rock and roll has undeclared dependencies. [[rosetta-tharpe]] played electric gospel guitar years before it had a name. [[rhythm-and-blues]] got its name in 1949. [[big-mama-thornton]] had a number-one "Hound Dog" three years before Elvis, and was paid by most accounts about $500. Then came [[rock-n-roll]], and later [[funk]], whose drum breaks would build hip-hop.

In 1955 the mother of [[emmett-till]] kept his coffin open, and the country saw. This chapter also has two copies. In one, [[tuskegee]]: for forty years the US Public Health Service watched Black men with syphilis go untreated. In the other, [[henrietta-lacks]]: cells taken from a 31-year-old woman without her knowledge are still dividing today.

Africa wrote back. [[achebe]] told an Igbo village from the inside in Things Fall Apart. [[tutuola]] wrote a Yoruba journey in his own English. [[ngugi]] wrote a novel in Gikuyu on prison toilet paper. In 1949 a man from Kankan created [[nko]], stung, the story goes, by a claim that African languages could not be written.

The music went political. [[afrobeat]] took shape when a Lagos bandleader read Malcolm X in Los Angeles. [[reggae]] got its name in Trench Town. In London, [[claudia-jones]] answered the 1958 riots with a carnival. [[radio-haiti]] gave the news in Creole and paid for it.

Not every story flatters. The [[paper-bag-test]] is about colorism inside Black communities, and [[stolen-legacy]] is about a myth that answered racism with another myth.
