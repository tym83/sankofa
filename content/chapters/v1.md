This release has no features. Count first: the [[slavevoyages]] database lists nearly 36,000 voyages, and the names of almost 100,000 Africans taken off the ships.

The trade had African partners as well. Kingdoms such as Dahomey and networks such as the Aro sold captives to European ships, and in 1999 the president of Benin asked forgiveness for it. That story is told in [[aro-and-dahomey]], with the same sources as everything else, and it does not make the Atlantic system smaller.

One of those taken, or so he wrote, was a boy from Igboland. [[equiano]] bought his freedom and wrote a bestseller, though two records say he was born in Carolina. In 1783 he brought a newspaper story to [[granville-sharp]]: on the [[zong]] more than 130 people had been thrown into the sea, and the owners claimed them as lost cargo.

Abolitionists answered with images. The plan of the [[brookes]] showed bodies packed like goods; the [[wedgwood-medallion]] asked "Am I Not a Man and a Brother?" and was worn as jewellery.

The law answered the other way. In Virginia, [[partus-1662]] made slavery pass from mother to child. [[anthony-johnson]], who arrived enslaved and became a free landowner, went to court in 1655 to keep a Black servant of his own. Long after the US banned the trade, the [[clotilda]] landed its captives in Alabama in 1860, and the survivors built their own African Town.
