People did not wait to be freed. In Luanda, [[nzinga]] refused a floor mat and sat on a kneeling attendant to meet the Portuguese governor eye to eye. In the hills of northeast Brazil, [[palmares]] held out for about a century. In Jamaica, the Windward Maroons of [[nanny-of-the-maroons]] forced the British to sign treaties.

In South Carolina, rebels marched with drums in 1739, and the colony banned drums, horns and loud instruments. The [[stono-drum-ban]] did not silence the rhythm; it moved into hands and bodies.

Then came the revolution no one in Europe thought possible. The [[haitian-revolution]] founded the only nation born of a successful slave rebellion, and France later made it pay for its own freedom. Some scholars think news of Haiti shaped Hegel's master and slave; the card says why that is contested.

Words travelled too. [[walkers-appeal]] was sewn into the linings of clothes and carried south. [[robert-smalls]] put on a captain's hat and steamed a Confederate ship past Fort Sumter. Harriet Tubman's [[combahee-raid]] carried more than 700 people out of slavery in one night.

Some famous stories are later inventions: the quilt code is one, and the [[underground-railroad-myths]] card sorts what is documented from what is not. Resistance did not stop at the Atlantic: in 1900 [[yaa-asantewaa]] led the siege of the British fort at Kumasi.
