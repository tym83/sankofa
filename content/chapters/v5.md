Long before Harlem, Black artists and thinkers were at the centre of European culture. In Granada, [[juan-latino|Juan Latino]] taught Latin and appears in the opening verses of Don Quixote. In 1729 [[amo|Anton Wilhelm Amo]] held a disputation on the rights of Africans in Europe; it is lost, and only a summary survives. In Paris, [[saint-georges|Joseph Bologne, Chevalier de Saint-Georges]] ran the orchestra that ordered Haydn's Paris symphonies. In Vienna in May 1803, Beethoven prepared and premiered his Sonata op. 47 with [[bridgetower|George Bridgetower]]; only later was it dedicated to Kreutzer. [[general-dumas|General Thomas-Alexandre Dumas]] was born to an enslaved woman in Saint-Domingue. On stage and in the concert hall, follow [[ira-aldridge|Ira Aldridge]] and [[burleigh-dvorak|Harry T. Burleigh, who sang spirituals for Antonín Dvořák]].

In 1900 W. E. B. Du Bois drew Black America in colour charts for Paris: [[du-bois-infographics|his data portraits]].

Then the music. [[jazz|Jazz]] came from New Orleans. In 1920 Mamie Smith recorded [[crazy-blues|"Crazy Blues"]], which opened the market for "race records", and the [[blues|blues]] reached the phonograph. Thomas A. Dorsey, a former blues pianist in Chicago, helped shape modern Black [[gospel|gospel]]. The [[chicago-defender|Chicago Defender]] called people north, and railroad porters smuggled it south. On the Gold Coast, [[highlife|highlife]] grew in the ballrooms.

Near Paris, in Clamart, [[nardal-sisters|Paulette and Jane Nardal]] held a salon with Sunday gatherings. It is often called a cradle of Négritude. And [[hurston|Zora Neale Hurston]] carried a Clotilda survivor's own words into a book that waited until 2018. In 1931 [[oliver-golden|Oliver Golden]] led a team of Black American agronomists to Soviet Uzbekistan.
