Long before Harlem, Black artists and thinkers were at the centre of European culture. In Granada, [[juan-latino]] taught Latin and turned up in the opening verses of Don Quixote. In Halle, [[amo]] argued that Africans could not lawfully be enslaved in Europe. In Paris, [[saint-georges]] ran the orchestra that ordered Haydn's Paris symphonies. In Vienna, Beethoven wrote his greatest violin sonata for [[bridgetower]].

Their stories moved into literature and art. [[general-dumas]], born to an enslaved woman in Saint-Domingue, gave his son the model for the prisoner of Monte Cristo. In St Petersburg, [[ira-aldridge]] broke into song while Taras Shevchenko drew him. In New York, spirituals sung by [[burleigh-dvorak|Harry Burleigh]] went into Dvořák's "New World".

In 1900 [[du-bois-infographics|W. E. B. Du Bois]] drew Black America in colour charts for Paris. A new music was spreading out of the American South: the [[blues]], first recorded by women. Mamie Smith's [[crazy-blues]] opened the market for "race records". [[jazz]] came from New Orleans, [[gospel]] from a former blues pianist in Chicago. The [[chicago-defender]] called people north, and railroad porters smuggled it south. On the Gold Coast, [[highlife]] grew in the ballrooms.

Paris had the Sunday salon of the [[nardal-sisters]], where Négritude began. [[hurston]] carried a Clotilda survivor's own words into a book that waited until 2018. And in 1931, [[oliver-golden]] took Black American cotton experts to Soviet Uzbekistan.
