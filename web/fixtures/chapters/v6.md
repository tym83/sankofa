On a December evening in 1955, a seasoned organiser sat on a bus in Montgomery
and did not stand up. [[rosa-parks|Rosa Parks]] had prepared for that moment
for twelve years.

Eight years later a quarter of a million people stood before the Lincoln
Memorial. At the [[march-on-washington]], a singer's call turned a speech into
a dream.

Not everyone wanted to sound hopeful. [[nina-simone]] answered the murders of
1963 with a song she could barely get on the radio.

In Kingston, independence came with a beat. Sound systems and studios made ska,
then [[reggae]], and sent it around the world and into the Bronx.

In Lagos, [[fela-kuti]] built afrobeat and declared his own republic. The army
answered with a raid; he answered with records.

From Montgomery to Lagos, the same years produced the same refusal in
different languages: a seat kept, a song not softened, a house declared free.
The Atlantic that had carried ships of captives now carried records, and the
records travelled in every direction.
