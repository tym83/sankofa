To write was to prove you were human, and the proof was demanded again and
again. [[phillis-wheatley]] published the first book of poems by an African
American in 1773, with a certificate from eighteen Boston gentlemen.

[[douglass|Frederick Douglass]] learned to read in secret and wrote a narrative
that forced him to flee the country. In the other edition of this museum, the
same road is walked by [[jacobs|Harriet Jacobs]], who hid for seven years under
a roof before she could write her own book.

Others spoke in public and were rewritten by others: compare the real and the
retold speeches of [[sojourner-truth]].

Songs carried what could not be printed. The [[spirituals]] travelled from the
Sea Islands to Queen Victoria and built a university hall.

In the 1930s the government sent writers to the last generation born in
slavery. The [[wpa-narratives]] hold more than 2,300 voices.

And one voice you can still hear: [[fountain-hughes]], recorded in Baltimore in
1949, remembering the day freedom came.
