In 1375 a mapmaker in Majorca drew the interior of Africa as he heard of it from
merchants: a crowned king on a throne, holding up a nugget of gold. The king was
[[mansa-musa]], and his pilgrimage to Mecca had made Mali famous from Cairo to
Catalonia.

His city of Timbuktu kept its learning in private libraries. The
[[timbuktu-manuscripts]] hold astronomy, law and poetry, and in 2012 their
keepers saved them by carrying them out in secret.

Far to the south, in the palace of the Oba, the casters of Benin recorded the
history of their court in brass. The [[benin-bronzes]] are now in museums on
three continents, and the question of where they belong is still open.

Across the continent, on the Zimbabwe plateau, a city of granite walls traded
gold to the Indian Ocean. [[great-zimbabwe]] was so impressive that colonial
rulers denied Africans had built it.

Africa wrote and spoke in many ways. In the Cross River valley, [[nsibidi]] signs
recorded secrets and judgements. Among the Yoruba, the [[talking-drum]] spoke
sentences that listeners understood as words.

This is the world the slave ships cut into. Remember it before you turn the
page.
