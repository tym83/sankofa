For three and a half centuries ships left the coast of Africa with people in
their holds. The [[middle-passage]] carried some 12.5 million, and most of what
we know about it comes from the paperwork of those who sold and insured them.

One survivor answered in his own words. [[equiano|Olaudah Equiano]] wrote his
life story, published it himself, and toured Britain until it had sold through
nine editions.

In 1783 he carried news of a ship called the Zong to the abolitionists. The
[[zong|court case]] that followed was about insurance, and it made Britain look
at what its trade had become.

The campaign against the trade invented its own media. The [[brookes]] diagram
packed hundreds of small figures into a printed hull, and the
[[wedgwood-medallion]] put a kneeling man on bracelets and snuffboxes.

Two centuries later a poet in Toronto took the Zong judgment apart word by word.
[[zong-philip|Zong!]] lets the drowned surface through the gaps in the record.
