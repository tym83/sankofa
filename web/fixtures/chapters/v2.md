Freedom was rarely granted. Most often it was taken. In the hills of
north-eastern Brazil, runaways built [[palmares]], a federation of villages that
held out against colonial armies for most of a century.

In the Blue Mountains of Jamaica, the Windward Maroons fought the British to a
treaty. Their leader, remembered as [[nanny-of-the-maroons|Queen Nanny]], is
the only woman among Jamaica's National Heroes.

In 1791 the enslaved of Saint-Domingue rose. The [[haitian-revolution]] defeated
three empires and ended in 1804 with the first state founded by people who had
freed themselves.

Its general, [[toussaint-louverture]], did not live to see independence. He died
in a French mountain fortress, and poets in England wrote to him.

In North America the road ran north. The [[underground-railroad]] was a network
of people, and its record keeper William Still wrote down hundreds of escapes.

Stories grew around it, and not all are true. The [[quilt-codes]] are a modern
legend; the real quilts deserve to be seen for what they are.
