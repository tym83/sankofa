In the twentieth century millions of people moved without asking anyone. The
[[great-migration]] took six million Black Southerners north and west, on trains
whose porters smuggled newspapers south.

They carried the [[blues]], first heard by a bandleader at a Mississippi station
and then plugged into amplifiers in Chicago.

In Harlem the new arrivals met writers, painters and musicians from across the
Black world. The [[harlem-renaissance]] made the neighbourhood a capital.

Its poet [[langston-hughes]] wrote verse in the rhythm of the blues and
[[jazz]], and travelled as far as Tashkent.

In a flat outside Paris, the [[nardal-sisters]] read Harlem's poets to students
from Africa and the Caribbean. The movement called Négritude began in
conversations like theirs.

And in 1938 a gospel singer with an electric guitar walked into the Cotton Club.
[[rosetta-tharpe|Sister Rosetta Tharpe]] is where rock and roll learned its
chords, an undeclared dependency that the music industry took decades to
acknowledge.
