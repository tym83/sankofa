Born Isabella Baumfree around 1797 in Ulster County, New York, she grew up
speaking Dutch. In 1826 she walked away from slavery carrying her baby
daughter, and in 1828 she sued to get back her son, who had been sold illegally
to Alabama, and won. In 1843 she renamed herself and began to preach. At Akron in
1851 she spoke for women's rights; the report printed that June has no "Ain't I
a woman?", a refrain that appeared in Frances Gage's retelling of 1863. To fund
her work she sold photographs of herself captioned "I sell the shadow to
support the substance". Like [[douglass]], she understood the camera.
