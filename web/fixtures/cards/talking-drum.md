The Yoruba dùndún is an hourglass drum laced with cords. Squeeze them under the
arm and the skin tightens and the note rises; relax and it falls. Because Yoruba
words are told apart by tone, a skilled drummer can recite praise names,
proverbs and news that listeners understand as speech. Akan drummers in the
Asante kingdom did the same on paired atumpan drums. Planters in the Americas
understood the danger: after the Stono rebellion, South Carolina's Negro Act of
1740 banned the enslaved from keeping drums and horns. The rhythm went into
hands, feet and voices instead, into [[spirituals]] and later [[jazz]].
