Born enslaved on the Bréda plantation near Cap-Français and freed in the 1770s,
Toussaint was nearly fifty when the [[haitian-revolution]] began. He read
Epictetus and military manuals, trained an army that beat the British, and by
1801 governed all of Hispaniola under a constitution he wrote, which abolished
slavery forever. Napoleon sent his brother-in-law to restore French rule. In
1802 Toussaint was invited to a meeting, arrested and shipped to Fort de Joux in
the Jura, where he died in April 1803. On the ship he is reported to have said the tree of
liberty had deep roots. Wordsworth's sonnet told him he had "great allies".
