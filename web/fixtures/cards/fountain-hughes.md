On 11 June 1949 in Baltimore, Hermond Norwood set up a disc recorder and talked
with Fountain Hughes, then about 101 years old. Hughes had been born enslaved in
Charlottesville, Virginia, and said his grandfather had belonged to Thomas
Jefferson. On the recording he speaks of being a boy when freedom came, of
having nothing, of a life of work in Baltimore. He says plainly that he would
rather die than be a slave again. It is one of a few dozen sound recordings of
formerly enslaved people kept by the Library of Congress, alongside the written
[[wpa-narratives]], and anyone can listen to it online.
