In 1324 the ruler of the Mali Empire crossed the Sahara on his way to Mecca with
a caravan that Arabic writers remembered for generations. Al-Umari, writing in
Cairo in the 1330s, heard that Musa had given away so much gold that its value
there had not recovered. Back home he brought scholars and the architect
al-Sahili, and Timbuktu grew into a city of mosques and books, the cradle of the
[[timbuktu-manuscripts]]. Fifty years later a mapmaker in Majorca painted him
on the Catalan Atlas, crowned and holding a golden nugget. The figures for his
wealth are later retellings and probably inflated, but the map shows what
Europe already knew: the gold came from West Africa.
