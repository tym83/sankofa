Kidnapped from West Africa as a child, she was sold in Boston in 1761 from a ship
called the Phillis and named after it. Within a few years she was reading Latin
and writing poems in heroic couplets. Boston printers would not publish an
enslaved African woman, so in 1773 she sailed to London, where her Poems on
Various Subjects appeared with her portrait and a signed attestation from
eighteen Boston gentlemen. It was the first book of poems by an African American.
She was freed soon after. In 1776 George Washington, whom she had praised in
verse, invited her to visit. Her line runs through [[douglass]] and
[[langston-hughes]].
