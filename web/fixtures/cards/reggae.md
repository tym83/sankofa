In the yards of Kingston in the 1950s, sound systems, trucks of speakers run by
rivals such as Duke Reid and Coxsone Dodd, played American rhythm and blues.
When the records ran out, Jamaican musicians made their own: ska around
independence in 1962, then the slower rocksteady, then reggae, named on record
by Toots and the Maytals in 1968. Rastafari gave it scripture; Bob Marley took it
around the world. In 1973 Clive Campbell, born in Kingston, threw a party in the
Bronx with his own sound system. In Lagos, [[fela-kuti]] was building another
sound from the same Atlantic.
