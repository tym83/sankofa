In 1787 the Society for Effecting the Abolition of the Slave Trade chose a seal:
a kneeling, chained African asking "Am I not a man and a brother?". Josiah
Wedgwood's modeller William Hackwood turned it into a small black-on-white
jasperware cameo, and Wedgwood gave them away. Thomas Clarkson later wrote that
ladies wore them as bracelets and hairpins, so that fashion for once served
justice. In 1788 Wedgwood sent a batch to Benjamin Franklin in Philadelphia.
Later artists and historians point out what the image leaves out: the people who
freed themselves, as in the [[haitian-revolution]], did not kneel. It sits
beside the [[brookes]] diagram as the first mass media of a movement.
