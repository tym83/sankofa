Son of the feminist campaigner Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti of Abeokuta, he studied
music in London, and on a tour of the United States in 1969 he met the Black
Power movement. Back in Lagos he fused Yoruba rhythm, highlife, jazz and James
Brown into afrobeat: long songs, big horns, lyrics in pidgin aimed at generals
and corruption. His compound, the Kalakuta Republic, refused the authority of the
state. After the album Zombie mocked the army, soldiers stormed it in 1977 and
threw his mother from a window; she died the next year. He carried a mock coffin
to the barracks. Compare [[reggae]] in Kingston.
