Between the 11th and 15th centuries the ancestors of the Shona built a capital
of granite on a hill above the Zimbabwe plateau. The outer wall of the Great
Enclosure rises about eleven metres, laid without mortar, with a chevron pattern
along the top. The city traded gold and ivory to the Swahili coast; Chinese
porcelain and Persian glass have been found in its ruins. European visitors
credited the Phoenicians or the Queen of Sheba. In 1929 the archaeologist
Gertrude Caton-Thompson showed it was African, and Rhodesia still pressed
guidebooks to blur the point. In 1980 the new country called itself Zimbabwe and
put the soapstone bird from these walls on its flag.
