On 1 December 1955 Rosa Parks refused to give up her seat on a Montgomery bus
and was arrested. She was 42 and had been secretary of the local NAACP since
1943; in 1944 she had gone to Abbeville to document the case of Recy Taylor, a
young mother assaulted by white men. That summer she had attended a workshop on
organising at the Highlander Folk School. In her own book she wrote that the
only tired she was, was tired of giving in. Black Montgomery walked for 381
days, and the boycott made a young pastor famous, as the [[march-on-washington]]
would show. She moved to Detroit, a city of the [[great-migration]], in 1957.
