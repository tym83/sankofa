Around 1903 the bandleader W. C. Handy, waiting for a late train at Tutwiler in
the Mississippi Delta, heard a man press a knife against his guitar strings and
sing about where the Southern crosses the Dog, two railway lines. Handy called it
the weirdest music he had ever heard and went on to publish blues in Memphis.
In 1920 Mamie Smith's Crazy Blues sold so well that record companies began
selling "race records". The music rode north with the [[great-migration]] and
plugged in on Chicago's South Side. Its line runs into [[jazz]],
[[langston-hughes|poetry]] and the guitar of [[rosetta-tharpe]].
