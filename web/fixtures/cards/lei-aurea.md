Article 1 declares slavery in Brazil extinct from the date of the law. Article 2
revokes everything to the contrary. Princess Isabel signed it in Rio de Janeiro
on 13 May 1888, but it was won by a movement: the lawyer Luiz Gama, once
enslaved himself, freed hundreds in court; the engineer André Rebouças and the
journalist José do Patrocínio campaigned; in 1881 the raft fishermen of
Fortaleza, led by Francisco José do Nascimento, refused to carry captives to the
ships, and Ceará abolished slavery in 1884. Brazil had received more captives
than any other country in the [[middle-passage]]. Days later, crowds filled Rio
for a thanksgiving mass.
