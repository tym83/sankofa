M. NourbeSe Philip, a poet born in Tobago and living in Toronto, set herself a
rule: use only the words of the legal report of [[zong|Gregson v Gilbert]], the
case about the ship Zong. She cut the judgment into syllables and scattered them
across the page, so that the names the court never recorded seem to surface
from under the text. The book lists invented African names for the dead along
the bottom of its pages. Philip performs it in day-long readings with
audiences. It is a way of hearing the [[middle-passage]] in the gaps of the
archive, the same way [[drexciya]] imagined it under the sea.
