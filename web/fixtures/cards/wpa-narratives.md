Between 1936 and 1938 the Federal Writers' Project, a New Deal relief program,
sent interviewers to the homes of elderly men and women in seventeen states.
More than 2,300 first-person accounts came back, with about 500 photographs, and
are now at the Library of Congress. They tell of work, food, songs, beatings,
escapes and the day freedom came. Read them carefully: most interviewers were
white, many wrote speech in heavy dialect, and some speakers were cautious with
strangers in the Jim Crow South. Folklorists also recorded voices on disc, such
as [[fountain-hughes]]. The narratives are the test that the [[quilt-codes]]
story fails.
