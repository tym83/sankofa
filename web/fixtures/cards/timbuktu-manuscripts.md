Astronomy tables, legal opinions, medical recipes, poetry, letters between
merchants: from the age of [[mansa-musa]] onward, Timbuktu's scholars and
families copied and kept books in Arabic and in African languages written in
Arabic script. The scholar Ahmed Baba, who died in 1627, said his own library
held 1,600 volumes and that it was the smallest in his family. The collections
stayed in private homes for centuries. When armed groups took the city in 2012,
the librarian Abdel Kader Haidara and his colleagues moved most of them south to
Bamako in secret, a few trunks at a time. Some thousands were burned; the
organisers say around 350,000 were saved.
