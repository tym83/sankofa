Joseph Bologne was born in Guadeloupe to a French planter and an enslaved woman,
Nanon, and brought to Paris as a boy. He became the finest fencer in France,
then a violinist, composer and conductor. He led the Concert des Amateurs and
the orchestra of the Loge Olympique, which commissioned Haydn's Paris
symphonies. When he was proposed as director of the Opéra, leading singers
objected to his colour, and the plan was dropped. In the Revolution he
commanded a regiment of men of colour, the Légion Saint-Georges, in Lille. A
generation later, [[ira-aldridge]] would cross Europe on another stage.
