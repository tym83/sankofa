She grew up in Eatonville, Florida, a town founded and governed by Black people,
and studied anthropology at Barnard with Franz Boas. She went south with a
notebook and a pistol to collect folktales, songs and hoodoo practice, and
published them in Mules and Men. In Africatown, near Mobile, she talked with
Kossola, called Cudjo Lewis, taken from West Africa on the ship Clotilda in
1860. Her book about him, Barracoon, written in his own speech, found no
publisher until 2018. Their Eyes Were Watching God was written in seven weeks in
Haiti. She died poor; Alice Walker found her unmarked grave in 1973.
