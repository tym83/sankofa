In the palace of the Oba of Benin, a guild of casters, the Igun Eronmwon, made
heads, figures and hundreds of brass plaques that recorded court life: chiefs,
musicians, warriors, and Portuguese traders with long hair and guns. Much of the
brass arrived as manillas, horseshoe-shaped bracelets used as currency in the
Atlantic trade; a 2023 study matched their chemistry to the Rhineland. In 1897 a
British force burned the palace and took thousands of these works, which were
sold across Europe. Since 2022 Germany and several American museums have
returned objects to Nigeria. The guild still works on Igun Street in Benin City.
