From the early 1600s people who escaped the sugar plantations of Pernambuco
built villages in the forested Serra da Barriga, in today's Alagoas. Palmares
grew into a federation of settlements with fields, forges, markets and a
king, Ganga Zumba. It repelled Dutch and Portuguese expeditions for
decades. Zumbi, who rejected a peace treaty, fought on until a colonial army
took the main town in 1694; he was killed on 20 November 1695. Brazil marks that
date as the Day of Black Consciousness, a national holiday since 2024. Its
cousins elsewhere include the Maroon towns of [[nanny-of-the-maroons|Jamaica]].
