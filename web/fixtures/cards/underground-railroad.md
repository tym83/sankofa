It was not a railway and not one organisation, but a network of people, most of
them Black, who moved fugitives north from the 1830s to the Civil War. Harriet
Tubman went back to Dorchester County, Maryland, again and again to lead people
out. In Philadelphia William Still wrote down the stories of hundreds of
arrivals, at the risk of prison. In 1850 one of them, Peter Still, told
of a mother who had fled long ago: William realised he was his brother.
[[douglass|Frederick Douglass]] and [[jacobs|Harriet Jacobs]] escaped by their
own routes. Later legends, like the [[quilt-codes]], grew around it.
