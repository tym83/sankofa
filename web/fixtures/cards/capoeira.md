It looks like a dance and works as a fight: two players in a ring of clapping
people, kicking, sweeping and cartwheeling to the berimbau, a one-string bow
with a gourd. The earliest pictures and police records come from Rio de Janeiro
in the early 1800s, where enslaved Africans played it in the streets; the
painter Rugendas drew it in 1835. After the [[lei-aurea|end of slavery]] the new
republic outlawed it. In Salvador da Bahia, Mestre Bimba opened an academy in
1932 and Mestre Pastinha defended the older Angola style, and from there it
spread to the world. Its roots in Africa, as with [[talking-drum|other
traditions]] of the [[middle-passage]], are argued over.
