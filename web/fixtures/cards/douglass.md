As a boy in Baltimore he traded bread with white children for reading lessons,
because his enslaver had forbidden anyone to teach him. In 1838 he escaped north
dressed as a sailor, carrying borrowed papers. His Narrative of 1845 named names
and places, so he had to flee to Britain until friends bought his freedom. He
edited a newspaper in Rochester, spoke against slavery and for women's suffrage,
and posed for photographers again and again, insisting on dignity in every
frame. He wrote that the sorrow songs, the [[spirituals]], were the truest
picture of slavery. In the other edition of this book, the same road to freedom
is walked by [[jacobs|Harriet Jacobs]].
