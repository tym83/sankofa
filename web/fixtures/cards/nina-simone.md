Eunice Waymon of Tryon, North Carolina, wanted to be the first Black concert
pianist in America and trained on Bach. When the Curtis Institute in
Philadelphia rejected her, she played bars in Atlantic City under a new name,
Nina Simone, mixing Bach counterpoint into jazz standards. In 1963, after the
murder of Medgar Evers and the bombing of a Birmingham church that killed four
girls, she wrote Mississippi Goddam in about an hour, and sang it at Carnegie
Hall in 1964. It was her first civil rights song. The movement of the
[[march-on-washington]] had found a voice that refused to be polite.
