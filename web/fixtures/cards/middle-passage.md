Between 1501 and 1866 some 12.5 million people were forced onto ships at ports
such as Luanda and Ouidah; about 10.7 million survived the crossing. The
largest share went to Brazil, then to the Caribbean; mainland North America
received about 390,000. These numbers come from ship by ship records collected
in the SlaveVoyages database, the voice of the [[zong|traders' own paperwork]].
Survivors carried what could not be confiscated: languages, prayers,
[[talking-drum|rhythms]], recipes, ways of fighting like [[capoeira]], and signs
like [[nsibidi]]. [[equiano|Olaudah Equiano]] wrote one of the few accounts of
the crossing by a person who survived it.
