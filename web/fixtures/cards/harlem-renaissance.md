After the First World War, Harlem filled with people from the
[[great-migration]] and the Caribbean, and for two decades it was a capital of
Black art. In 1925 Alain Locke's anthology The New Negro announced a generation:
[[langston-hughes|Langston Hughes]], [[zora-neale-hurston|Zora Neale Hurston]],
Countee Cullen, Aaron Douglas. Arturo Schomburg, born in San Juan, had collected
books, manuscripts and prints proving that Black people had a history; in 1926
the New York Public Library bought his collection, which grew into the
Schomburg Center. Across the ocean, the [[nardal-sisters]] read Harlem's poets in
Paris and passed them on.
