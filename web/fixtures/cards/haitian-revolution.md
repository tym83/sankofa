In August 1791 the enslaved of the northern plain of Saint-Domingue, France's
richest colony, rose and burned the plantations. Over thirteen years they
defeated French, Spanish and British armies and, in 1802, Napoleon's expedition.
Leaders such as [[toussaint-louverture]] and Jean-Jacques Dessalines turned a
revolt into a state. On 1 January 1804 at Gonaïves, Haiti declared independence,
the first nation founded by people who had freed themselves from slavery. The
only known printed copy of the declaration turned up in a British archive in
2010. Haitians mark the day with soup joumou, the soup they were once forbidden
to eat.
