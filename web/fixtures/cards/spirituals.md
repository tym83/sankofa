Sung in fields, at night meetings and in praise houses, spirituals joined
African call and response with Bible stories of Moses, Jordan and the promised
land. [[douglass|Frederick Douglass]] warned that they were not songs of
contentment but of sorrow. The first collection, Slave Songs of the United
States, was gathered on the Sea Islands of South Carolina in 1867. In 1871 nine
students from Fisk University in Nashville set out on tour to save their school.
They sang for Queen Victoria in 1873, and the money built Jubilee Hall. From
here run lines to the [[blues]], gospel and [[rosetta-tharpe|Sister Rosetta
Tharpe]].
