In 1788 abolitionists in Plymouth printed a plan of the Liverpool ship Brookes,
and the London committee reissued it in 1789: rows of small figures packed
head to foot, 482 people on the decks. It was drawn to the limit allowed by a
new law; on earlier voyages the ship had carried far more. Thomas Clarkson took
copies to Paris, and Mirabeau had a model built to show the National Assembly.
The image was printed in pamphlets, newspapers and posters on both sides of the
Atlantic. Like the [[wedgwood-medallion]], it spoke for people who were not
allowed to speak; artists today redraw it to give them faces.
