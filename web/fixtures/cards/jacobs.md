To escape her enslaver in Edenton, North Carolina, Harriet Jacobs hid in a space
under the roof of her grandmother's shed, about three feet high at its peak. She
stayed there from 1835 to 1842, watching her children through a hole she bored
in the wall and sewing by the light that came through it. Then she escaped by
ship to the North. In 1861 she published Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl
under the name Linda Brent, one of the first books to speak openly of the
sexual abuse of enslaved women. In 1981 Jean Fagan Yellin proved from letters
that Jacobs wrote it herself. Her counterpart in the other edition is
[[douglass|Frederick Douglass]].
