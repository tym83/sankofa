In 1920, travelling to see his father in Mexico, the young Hughes looked out at
the Mississippi at sunset and wrote a poem about the Euphrates, the Congo, the
Nile and that river; it was printed in The Crisis in 1921. He then set poems to
the rhythms of the [[blues]] and [[jazz]], which critics first called low and
later called modern. He was the voice of the [[harlem-renaissance]] for forty
years. In 1932 he went to the Soviet Union for a film that was never made, and
stayed to travel through Central Asia, writing about Tashkent and Samarkand. He
answered [[phillis-wheatley]] across a century and a half.
