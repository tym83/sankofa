On Sunday afternoons the enslaved and free people of colour of New Orleans
gathered at Congo Square to dance. In 1819 the architect Benjamin Latrobe wrote
down what he saw and drew the instruments. By 1900 the city's brass bands,
church music, ragtime and [[blues]] had mixed into something new, played by
Buddy Bolden and then by a boy from the back of town, Louis Armstrong. The
first jazz record, in 1917, was made by a white band, but Armstrong's Hot Five
recordings in Chicago in the 1920s showed the world what a soloist could do.
Jazz went north with the [[great-migration]] and into the verse of
[[langston-hughes]].
