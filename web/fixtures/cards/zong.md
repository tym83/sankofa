In late 1781 the crew of the Liverpool ship Zong, short of water after a
navigation error, threw more than 130 sick Africans into the sea off Jamaica.
The owners then claimed insurance for them as lost property. When the insurers
refused, the case reached Lord Mansfield in 1783, and the judges discussed
drowned people as if they were horses. No one was charged with murder.
[[equiano|Olaudah Equiano]] carried the news to Granville Sharp, who paid for a
shorthand transcript of the hearing and sent it to the Admiralty. That record
helped turn Britain against the trade. Two centuries later it became raw
material for a poem, [[zong-philip|Zong!]].
